Build a time machine, and according to Stephen Hawking, the universe may destroy it before you switch it on.

Start with a strange fact. Empty space is not really empty. Even in a perfect vacuum, tiny quantum fluctuations flicker in and out of existence. Normally they are harmless, and they cancel each other out.

Now imagine you try to build a time machine out of a wormhole, a hypothetical tunnel between two places in spacetime. You hold it open and set one end so that it connects to an earlier moment. Everything depends on what happens to those fluctuations.

Here is the problem. A fluctuation can enter the tunnel, travel into the past, and come out at the very moment it entered. Then it can go around again, and again. Each time it passes, it adds energy to the system.

Think of a microphone held too close to a loudspeaker. The sound goes in, comes out louder, goes back in, and in a split second you get a painful screech. That is feedback.

Calculations by physicists, including Hawking, suggest that spacetime could suffer a similar feedback near the edge of a time machine. The energy appears to build up without limit, right at the boundary where backward travel would begin. That boundary is called a chronology horizon.

If that is right, the wormhole would be torn apart before any traveler could step inside. Nature would blow up the bridge before anyone could cross it.

In 1992, Hawking turned this idea into the Chronology Protection Conjecture. He wrote that there seems to be a chronology protection agency that keeps the universe safe for historians.

Now for the honest part. This is a conjecture, not a proof. The calculations treat gravity as smooth and only the fluctuations as quantum. Close to the horizon, that method stops working. So a complete theory of quantum gravity, which does not exist yet, could change the answer.

That is why physicists still argue about it. Some think the protection is real. Others think a future theory may allow a loophole. No one has built a wormhole, and no one has seen a time machine.
