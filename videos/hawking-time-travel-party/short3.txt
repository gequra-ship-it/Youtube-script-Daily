You travel back in time and stop your grandparents from meeting. Now, who built the machine?

Physicists call this the grandfather paradox, and it is the reason many of them distrust time travel to the past.

Follow the chain. If your grandparents never meet, your parents are never born. If your parents are never born, you are never born. But if you were never born, you could not have traveled back in time to stop them. The story cancels itself.

This is not just a clever puzzle. It strikes at the foundation of science, which is causality. Causality means a cause must come before its effect. You strike a match, and then the flame appears. You drop an egg, and then it breaks.

If an effect could come before its cause, the universe would stop making sense. A broken egg could put itself back together and jump into your hand. No stable laws of nature could exist in a world where the future can erase its own past.

This is one reason Stephen Hawking argued that nature must protect itself from backward time travel. In 1992, he proposed the Chronology Protection Conjecture, the idea that the laws of physics prevent time machines from forming. Remember that it is a conjecture, not a proven fact.

Physicists have tried to escape the paradox in several ways. One idea, from the Russian physicist Igor Novikov, says that if loops in time exist, only self-consistent histories can happen. You could travel to the past, but you could never change it. Another idea is parallel worlds. If you change the past, you create a new branch of the universe instead of altering your own.

Both ideas are speculation. No experiment supports either one. And the parallel-world escape has a cost. You would arrive in a different timeline, forever cut off from the world you left.

Here is the mathematical background. General relativity does allow some paths that loop back in time. Physicists call them closed timelike curves. In 1949, Kurt Gödel found a rotating universe that would contain them. But astronomers have never seen our universe rotate that way, so his universe is not ours.
