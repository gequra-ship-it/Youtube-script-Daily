Topic: "Fifteen Years on a Sandbank". 1761: the French East India Company ship Utile (a wooden three-masted sailing ship, 18th century) runs onto a coral reef at night near Tromelin, a tiny flat island of white sand and coral in the Indian Ocean (about 1,700 m long, 700 m wide, highest point about 7 m, NO trees, no river, a few low dunes). The French crew build a small boat (the Providence) from the wreck and sail away; it cannot carry the Malagasy people (about sixty) who must stay; a promised rescue ship does not come. They survive 15 years: a well dug in the sand, sooty terns and eggs, sea turtles, fish, a fire that never goes out (driftwood and wreck timber, flint and striker), low buildings of upright beach-sandstone slabs topped with coral blocks, copper bowls hammered and repaired, a small craft that sails away and is never seen again. Archaeologists (team led by a former naval officer, digs 2006/2008/2010/2013: bird bones, copper bowls, flint, nails). Nov 29 1776: corvette La Dauphine, young officer Tromelin, a boat through the surf; 7 women and an 8-month-old baby boy rescued; freed; baby named Jacques Moyse; island later named Tromelin. Closing: hope is a flame best kept alive together.
Visuals: ocean map with a tiny speck, flat white-sand island with coral ring and reef surf, night sea, 18th-century wooden sailing ship (hull, masts, square sails), reef breaking a hull, survivors on a beach at sunrise (tiny gentle figures), dug well, seabird colony, turtle laying eggs, campfire with driftwood, stone buildings, copper bowl, archaeologist digging with brushes and a trowel, sieve, bird bones, a small boat and a raft on the horizon, empty horizon, sailing ship (corvette) with lantern, rescue boat in surf, seven women and a baby on the sand, a calendar/hourglass for years, map of Madagascar and Mauritius. Closing scenes: a small fire on a lonely shore.
