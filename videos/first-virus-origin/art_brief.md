Topic: "Where Did the First Virus Come From?": viruses are inert outside a host (no metabolism, no energy, like dust) and hijack a cell to print copies; are they alive? Three hypotheses: VIRUS-FIRST (young boiling Earth 3.5+ billion years ago, RNA world, self-copying RNA chains gaining protein shells in an ancient sea), REDUCTION (a small cell living inside a bigger cell discards genes one by one until only a minimal code in a shell remains; giant viruses Mimivirus and Pandoravirus, found in a cooling tower and in coastal sediment off Chile, bigger than some bacteria, visible in a light microscope, ~1000-2000+ genes, maybe master gene thieves), ESCAPE (jumping genes / mobile DNA cut and paste inside a genome; a rogue piece builds a capsid and leaves the cell). Many origins: viruses may have no single ancestor. Viral fossil record: no mineral fossils, but endogenous retroviruses stitched into sperm/egg DNA = ~8% of human DNA; syncytin gene recycled for the placenta; ocean: tens of millions of viruses per milliliter of seawater, viruses burst bacteria and plankton, releasing carbon/nitrogen/phosphorus (viral shunt), gene swapping between species.
Visuals: microscope field, lab bench, petri dish, a kitchen counter with a lifeless virus speck, a living cell with a lock/receptor and a key-shaped virus, cell factory printing copies, young Earth with boiling sea, volcanoes and lightning, RNA ribbon chains, small cell inside a bigger cell, shrinking gene-strip, giant virus next to a bacterium for scale, cooling tower, coastal sediment, DNA helix with jumping gene segments, capsid wrapped around a gene piece, family-tree with many roots, stone/bone fossils versus a DNA strand fossil, sperm and egg, placenta metaphor, seawater drop under magnification, plankton and bacteria bursting into glowing nutrient dots, ocean food web, a winter cold and a sneeze (calm, no gore), hydrothermal vent and ancient shoreline at the end.
