=== 한국어 요약 (전체 번역이 아닌 요약본) ===
■ 영상 제목: The Terrifying Science That Breaks the Hulk (헐크를 무너뜨리는 무시무시한 과학)

■ 한 줄 요약: 만화 속 헐크의 변신과 초인적 능력은 실제 물리학과 생물학 법칙(감마선 피폭, 질량 보존, 열역학, 제곱-세제곱 법칙, 순환계 한계 등)을 적용할 경우 즉각적인 신체 붕괴와 사망으로 이어진다.

■ 전체 흐름 (대본 순서대로 8~15줄):
- 감마선 피폭은 인간에게 초능력을 주는 것이 아니라 DNA를 조각내고 세포 분열을 막아 장기 부전을 일으킨다.
- 70킬로그램(kg)의 브루스 배너(Bruce Banner)가 500킬로그램(kg) 이상으로 변할 때, 질량 보존 법칙상 400킬로그램(kg)의 질량이 무에서 생겨날 수 없다.
- 순수 에너지를 질량으로 변환하려면 도시 전체를 증발시킬 막대한 에너지가 필요하며, 질량이 그대로라면 밀도가 스티로폼 수준으로 떨어져 바람에 날아가게 된다.
- 3초 만에 수백 킬로그램의 근육과 뼈를 합성하면 발생하는 극단적인 열역학적 폐열로 인해 체액이 끓어올라 증발한다.
- 갈릴레오 갈릴레이(Galileo Galilei)의 제곱-세제곱 법칙(square-cube law)에 따라 9피트(nine-foot)로 거대화되면 체중을 지탱하지 못해 뼈가 부서진다.
- 공중으로 높이 도약할 때의 반작용으로 발뼈와 관절이 파열되며, 착지 충격으로 뇌가 두개골 안쪽에 부딪혀 치명상을 입는다.
- 거대한 몸과 뇌에 피를 보내기 위해 심혈관 수압이 폭증하면서 뇌 모세혈관이 파열되어 출혈성 뇌졸중(hemorrhagic stroke)을 겪는다.
- 거대 근육을 유지할 산소 부족으로 젖산이 급격히 쌓이고, 3초 만에 4배로 늘어나는 피부는 장력을 견디지 못해 찢어진다.
- 체적 대비 표면적 감소로 체열을 방출하지 못해 체온이 치솟고, 탱크를 들어 올리는 근력을 쓰면 힘줄이 뼈에서 뜯겨 나간다.
- 극단적인 분노로 인한 호르몬 과다로 신경계가 손상되며, 시간당 수천 칼로리를 소모하는 대사량을 감당하지 못해 저혈당 쇼크(hypoglycemic coma)로 쓰러진다.
- 헐크는 인간 감정의 폭발을 표현한 은유이며, 실제로는 자연법칙을 거스르지 않고 정교한 균형을 유지하는 인간의 몸 자체가 가장 위대한 경이로움이다.

■ 핵심 사실·숫자 (5~10개):
- 브루스 배너의 기본 체중은 약 70킬로그램(kg), 변신 후 체중은 500킬로그램(kg) 이상으로 400킬로그램(kg) 이상의 질량 차이가 발생함.
- 물질 1킬로그램(kg)을 순수 에너지로부터 직접 생성하는 데는 약 90경 줄(ninety quadrillion joules)의 에너지가 필요함.
- 변신 시 키는 9피트(nine-foot) 거인으로 확장되며, 이 과정이 3초 만에 일어남.
- 갈릴레오 갈릴레이(Galileo Galilei)가 300여 년 전 제시한 제곱-세제곱 법칙에 따르면, 키가 2배가 될 때 표면적은 4배, 부피와 질량은 8배로 증가함.
- 일반 성인의 심장 무게는 약 300그램(g)이며, 휴식 시 분당 5리터(L)의 혈액을 펌프질함.
- 9피트 거인의 거대한 이두근을 유지하려면 농구장 크기의 폐포 표면적과 하수관 수준 너비의 기도가 필요함.
- 피부 표면적이 3초 만에 4배로 늘어나면 피부의 인장 탄성 한계를 초과하여 파열됨.
- 변신 후 거대한 근육을 유지하기 위해 휴식 상태에서도 시간당 수천 칼로리(calories)를 소모함.

■ 숏츠 5편:
- 숏츠 1: Where Does the Hulk's Extra Mass Come From? (헐크의 추가 질량은 어디서 오는가?) — 70킬로그램의 브루스 배너가 3초 만에 500킬로그램 이상의 거인으로 변신하는 것은 질량 보존 법칙에 위배됩니다. 에너지를 질량으로 변환하려면 약 36해 줄(thirty-six quintillion joules)의 에너지가 필요해 주변 도시를 증발시키게 됩니다. 만약 질량이 그대로라면 밀도가 스티로폼 수준으로 급감해 바람에 날아가 버리고 맙니다.
- 숏츠 2: What Gamma Rays Actually Do to Human DNA (감마선이 인간의 DNA에 실제로 미치는 영향) — 전자기파 스펙트럼 중 가장 에너지가 높은 감마선은 초능력을 깨우는 것이 아니라 세포 조직을 파괴합니다. 감마선은 DNA 이중나선을 조각내 세포 분열 능력을 완전히 파괴합니다. 실제 피폭 시 소화관 내벽이 녹아내리고 면역세포가 사라지며 수 시간 내에 전신 장기 부전으로 사망하게 됩니다.
- 숏츠 3: The Deadly Heat Behind an Instant Transformation (즉각적인 변신 뒤에 숨겨진 치명적인 열) — 수백 킬로그램의 근육과 뼈를 3초 만에 합성하려면 수백만 개의 생화학 반응이 초고속으로 일어나야 합니다. 이 과정에서 발생하는 열역학적 폐열은 생명체에게 치명적인 수준에 도달합니다. 결과적으로 체온이 물의 끓는점을 넘어 치솟으면서 체액이 고압 증기로 변해 내부에서부터 삶아지게 됩니다.
- 숏츠 4: Why the Square-Cube Law Crushes Giant Superheroes (제곱-세제곱 법칙이 거대 슈퍼히어로를 무너뜨리는 이유) — 갈릴레오의 제곱-세제곱 법칙에 따르면 키가 2배로 커질 때 체중은 8배로 늘어납니다. 9피트 거인이 인간의 신체 비율을 그대로 유지하면 자신의 체중조차 지탱하지 못해 뼈가 부서집니다. 또한 공중 도약 시 발목과 무릎이 파열되고, 착지 충격으로 인해 두개골 속의 뇌가 으깨지게 됩니다.
- 숏츠 5: Why a Giant Heart Would Instantly Explode (거대한 심장이 즉시 폭발하는 이유) — 9피트 거인의 뇌까지 피를 공급하려면 기린처럼 극도로 높은 심장 수압이 필요합니다. 거대한 근육망에 혈액을 보내기 위해 심장이 산업용 펌프처럼 강하게 뛰면 혈관이 터지게 됩니다. 급증한 동맥압은 뇌 모세혈관을 파열시켜 전투를 시작하기도 전에 치명적인 출혈성 뇌졸중을 일으킵니다.

[영상 제목]: The Terrifying Science That Breaks the Hulk

=== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 ===

Take a look at your hands resting quietly in front of you. When you feel a sudden surge of frustration, your palms might sweat, and your heart might beat a little faster. In fiction, this familiar emotional spark triggers something extraordinary. A mild scientist loses his temper, his skin turns vibrant green, and his body violently expands into a towering nine-foot giant. We have watched this spectacular transformation on screen for decades, cheering as concrete crumbles beneath his feet. But what would actually happen if real biology and physics tried to obey that comic book command? 

The origin of this legendary transformation begins with an invisible blast of gamma radiation. In the comic book universe, gamma rays act like a cosmic sculptor, waking up dormant genetic potential and granting godlike durability. In our physical reality, gamma rays are the most energetic form of light in the entire electromagnetic spectrum. They do not rewrite your genetic code to give you thicker muscles or indestructible green skin. Instead, they slice clean through your living tissues like microscopic daggers, violently stripping electrons from your delicate molecular structures. 

Have you ever wondered what real ionizing radiation does to human chromosomes? It shatters the delicate ladder of your DNA into countless broken fragments, completely overwhelming your cellular repair teams. If a living person received a sudden massive dose of gamma radiation, their cells would instantly lose the ability to divide. Within hours, the delicate lining of their stomach would dissolve, their white blood cells would vanish, and organ failure would begin. Gamma rays do not create invincible monsters; they break the machinery of life down to silent dust. 

Even if we magically ignore the fatal radiation, we immediately slam into the most unyielding rule of our universe: the conservation of mass. Bruce Banner is typically portrayed as a slender man weighing roughly seventy kilograms. When he transforms into his hulking alter ego, his body suddenly weighs well over five hundred kilograms. Where does those extra four hundred kilograms of physical matter actually come from? Mass cannot simply appear out of thin air just because an emotional threshold has been crossed. 

Could that extra mass be converted directly from pure energy? Albert Einstein showed us that mass and energy are two sides of the same coin through his famous formula. But converting pure energy into four hundred kilograms of flesh and bone would require an astronomical amount of energy. Scientists know that creating just a single kilogram of physical matter requires roughly ninety quadrillion joules. Pulling hundreds of kilograms into existence in three seconds would unleash enough raw power to vaporize the entire surrounding city. 

What if his total mass never actually changes during the transformation? If Bruce Banner expands to three times his original volume while keeping the exact same seventy kilograms, something hilarious and strange occurs. His physical density would plummet to something comparable to styrofoam or dry cork. Instead of shaking the pavement with thunderous steps, a gust of autumn wind could easily send him tumbling across the parking lot. He would not smash armored tanks; he would bounce harmlessly off their windshields like an oversized carnival balloon. 

Now, let us assume for a moment that mass could somehow be gathered instantly from surrounding atmospheric molecules. The chemical assembly line inside living cells operates under strict thermodynamic rules. Building proteins, cellular membranes, and dense bone tissue requires thousands of metabolic reactions occurring in perfect sequence. In an ordinary human body, synthesizing just a few grams of new muscle tissue takes weeks of intense nutrition and cellular labor. Forcing that entire architectural process to occur within three seconds would break the speed limits of organic chemistry. 

Every single biochemical reaction inside your body releases a tiny amount of waste heat. When you sprint up a flight of stairs, your temperature rises noticeably as your muscles consume fuel. What happens when hundreds of kilograms of living flesh are synthesized in the blink of an eye? The immense thermodynamic waste heat generated by that metabolic burst would be utterly catastrophic. Instead of roaring triumphantly at his enemies, our transformed hero would instantly boil from the inside out, turning his own water into high-pressure steam. 

Let us look past the transformation itself and examine the physical mechanics of a nine-foot humanoid body. More than three centuries ago, Galileo Galilei introduced a principle known as the square-cube law. When you double the height of an object, its surface area increases by four times, but its volume and mass multiply by eight. This simple mathematical truth places a strict structural limit on every living creature on Earth. A human scaled up to giant proportions does not simply become stronger; he becomes overwhelmingly heavy. 

Why do large animals like elephants look so fundamentally different from agile gazelles? An elephant requires thick, vertical, pillar-like legs placed directly beneath its center of mass to support its immense tonnage. It cannot leap gracefully over fences, nor can it sprint across uneven ground without risking catastrophic skeletal collapse. If a humanoid giant retained human proportions while weighing half a ton, his structural integrity would be severely compromised. His thigh bones would experience crushing compressive stress just trying to stand upright on a flat surface. 

Consider what happens when our green giant decides to leap miles across a desert landscape. In stories, he bends his knees, launches into the stratosphere, and leaves a crater in the bedrock. But every physical action demands an equal and opposite reaction through the laws of motion. The kinetic force required to propel five hundred kilograms into the sky would generate immense mechanical pressure against his own foot bones. His ankles and knees would experience forces that far exceed the breaking point of any known biological mineral. 

Landing after that heroic leap presents an even more devastating mechanical challenge. As he plummets from the clouds, gravity accelerates his massive bulk toward the unforgiving ground. The sudden deceleration upon impact transfers billions of foot-pounds of kinetic energy straight through his skeletal frame. Even if his external skin were as tough as reinforced steel, internal organs are soft, watery cushions. His brain would smash violently against the inner wall of his skull, producing instantaneous trauma long before he could throw a punch. 

What kind of cardiovascular system could keep such a monstrous biological engine alive? In an ordinary adult, the heart weighs about three hundred grams and pumps five liters of blood per minute at a modest resting pressure. To pump rich arterial blood to the brain of a nine-foot giant, the heart must generate immense hydrostatic pressure. Consider the giraffe, an animal that evolved exceptionally thick muscular heart walls and specialized pressure-regulating valves just to pump blood up its long neck without fainting. 

If a human heart suddenly had to pump thick blood through a massive muscular network, the required pressure would rupture standard human blood vessels. The heart itself would need to swell to the size of a large medicine ball, beating with enormous hydraulic force. Yet, if that blood pressure spiked as fast as his rage, the delicate capillary networks inside his cerebral cortex would burst instantly. Long before entering battle, the towering creature would suffer a massive hemorrhagic stroke, collapsing unconscious onto the shattered pavement. 

How would such an enormous engine supply itself with vital oxygen? Muscle tissue requires an uninterrupted flow of oxygen molecules to break down carbohydrates and maintain forceful contraction. A giant body with enormous muscle fibers would consume oxygen at a rate that would leave standard mammalian lungs completely starved. To keep those massive biceps flexing, his lungs would need an internal surface area the size of an entire basketball court, paired with windpipes as wide as sewer pipes. Without that impossible airflow, his muscles would lock up with agonizing lactic acid within seconds. 

Then there is the quiet mystery of the skin itself, which somehow expands without tearing into shreds. Human skin is a remarkably resilient organ, capable of gentle stretching over months of growth or pregnancy. But living dermal tissue has finite tensile elasticity and cannot expand four times its surface area in three seconds. In the real world, such rapid expansion would rip capillaries, tear epidermal layers, and result in catastrophic internal and external bleeding. His legendary green armor would simply peel apart under the sheer speed of expansion. 

Have you ever considered how large animals manage to keep their internal organs cool? Small animals like mice lose heat so quickly they must eat constantly just to avoid freezing, while large animals struggle constantly to dump excess thermal energy. As an organism grows larger, its surface area relative to its volume shrinks dramatically. A massive humanoid giant, generating immense heat from hundreds of pounds of furious muscle, would have almost no efficient way to sweat or radiate that thermal energy away. Within minutes of combat, his internal temperature would soar past fatal thresholds. 

What about the mechanical connections that tie muscle to bone? Skeletal muscles do not pull directly on bone; they pull through fibrous cords called tendons. Tendons are magnificent biological shock absorbers, but they possess clear mechanical limits under shearing stress. If a creature truly generated enough muscular force to lift a main battle tank, the sheer tension would tear those tendons cleanly off their bony anchors. Real physics dictates that before you can crush an armored vehicle, your own internal anchor points must be capable of withstanding the equal recoil. 

Behind all this physical muscle lies the complex chemistry of raw emotional anger. In the human brain, anger is governed by the amygdala, triggering a flood of adrenaline, noradrenaline, and cortisol. This survival mechanism is designed for brief, explosive bursts of escape or defense, not prolonged warfare. Sustaining that extreme level of neurological and hormonal activation would fry the delicate receptors of the nervous system. The brain would drown in its own neurochemical flood, resulting in rapid cognitive exhaustion, severe tremors, and profound physiological shock. 

How many calories would this living war machine require just to remain standing on the battlefield? A human body carrying several hundred kilograms of active muscle would possess an astronomical basal metabolic rate. Even resting quietly, such a body would burn through thousands of calories every single hour. To sustain a prolonged fight, our hero would need to consume mountains of calorie-dense food almost constantly. Without a massive and continuous supply of glucose, his blood sugar would plummet, his brain would shut down, and he would collapse into a hypoglycemic coma. 

Why do comic books and cinematic blockbusters ignore these unbending physical realities? Because stories are not meant to be textbooks on bioenergetics or mechanical engineering. The image of a fragile man bursting through his physical constraints is an enduring metaphor for the raw power of human emotion. We love the fantasy because every one of us has felt anger so overwhelming that our ordinary bodies felt far too small to contain it. The green giant is not a portrait of real biology; he is a portrait of the untamed psychological storms that brew within us all. 

When you return to your own quiet reflection, the truth becomes far more fascinating than any painted superhero. Your bones are not reinforced with mythical gamma energy, yet they are lighter and stronger relative to their weight than many industrial materials. Your heart maintains a gentle, unwavering rhythm for decades, adjusting its hydraulic pressure with microscopic precision every time you stand up or sit down. Your skin stretches, breathes, and heals itself without violating a single rule of the physical universe. 

Real biology does not rely on sudden, violent bursts of mass or impossible thermal fires to accomplish miracles. Instead, it relies on millions of years of patient evolution, balancing every muscle fiber, blood vessel, and nerve impulse in delicate, breathtaking harmony. True strength does not come from tearing through the laws of nature with blinding fury. The quiet, balanced machinery keeping you alive this very second is the greatest marvel the universe has ever built.
