How could a group that was so dominant and geographically widespread go completely extinct, while its seemingly identical relative clung to survival? Today, we will uncover the hidden biological secrets that decided their fates.

- 영어 자막: Beneath the Spiral Shell
- 영어 TTS: To solve this mystery, we must first abandon the assumption that superficial resemblance equals biological kinship. Despite their matching coiled shells, ammonites and nautiluses actually belong to distinct branches within the cephalopod family tree. The nautilus belongs to the ancient subclass Nautiloidea, which branched off extremely early in evolutionary time. Surprisingly, ammonites are much more closely related to modern soft-bodied cephalopods like squids and octopuses than they are to nautiluses. Their similar external appearance is actually a textbook example of convergent evolution.

This specialized biological tube allows the animal to regulate gas and liquid inside the chambers, effectively functioning like a submarine's ballast tank. However, the precise location of this vital organ differs drastically between the two groups. In a nautilus, the siphuncle runs directly through the dead center of each chamber wall, providing symmetrical structural stability.

For decades, scientists fiercely debated why ammonites evolved such breathtakingly complex internal reinforcements. Some argued that these fractal flutings allowed them to withstand external hydrostatic pressure while keeping their shells thin and lightweight for active swimming. Others hypothesized that the complex edges helped maximize the surface area for rapid liquid drainage during rapid depth changes. Regardless of their exact purpose, these specialized structures reveal a creature heavily adapted for high performance.

- 영어 TTS: This brings us straight to the crucial question of habitat preference and lifestyle. Ammonites were essentially the undisputed rockstars of the sunlit upper ocean layer, known technically as the epipelagic zone. Hundreds of fossil specimens recovered worldwide indicate that most ammonite species inhabited water depths between zero and one hundred meters. They thrived where warm sunlight penetrated freely, supporting massive swarms of microscopic life and small fishes. Their hydrodynamic shell shapes and sophisticated buoyancy mechanisms allowed them to operate as agile, active hunters within these bustling coastal shallows.
- 한글 번역: 이는 서식지 선호도와 생활 방식이라는 매우 중요한 질문으로 우리를 곧바로 이끕니다. 암모나이트는 기술적으로 '표해수대'로 알려진 햇빛이 쏟아지는 상층부 바다의 확고한 주인공들이었습니다. 전 세계에서 발견된 수백 개의 화석 표본은 대부분의 암모나이트 종이 수심 0미터에서 100미터 사이의 얕은 바다에 서식했음을 일관되게 보여줍니다. 그들은 따뜻한 햇빛이 자유롭게 침투하여 미세 생물 무리와 작은 물고기들이 번성하는 환경에서 번영을 누렸습니다. 유선형 껍데기와 정교한 부력 메커니즘 덕분에 활기찬 연안 천해에서 민첩하고 활동적인 사냥꾼으로 활약할 수 있었습니다.

- 영어 TTS: Modern nautiluses, on the other hand, tell a completely different geographic and ecological story. Today, nautiluses are predominantly deep-water dwellers, inhabiting the shadowy twilight zone of coral reef drop-offs between two hundred and seven hundred meters down. Their thick, rigid shells are essentially heavy-duty pressure vessels designed to withstand immense ambient pressures that would instantly crush an automobile. They deliberately avoid warm surface waters because their primitive circulatory systems can easily experience physiological failure in elevated temperatures. This striking difference in vertical depth distribution was not accidental.
- 한글 번역: 반면에 현대의 앵무조개는 완전히 다른 지리적, 생태학적 이야기를 들려줍니다. 오늘날 앵무조개는 주로 수심 200미터에서 700미터에 이르는 산호초 절벽의 어스름한 심해 박명층에 서식하는 깊은 바다 주민입니다. 그들의 두껍고 단단한 껍데기는 자동차를 순식간에 찌그러뜨릴 만한 엄청난 수압을 견디도록 설계된 튼튼한 압력 용기나 다름없습니다. 앵무조개는 높은 온도에서 원시적인 순환계가 생리적 기능 장애를 일으키기 때문에 따뜻한 표층수를 극도로 기피합니다. 이러한 수직적 서식 수심의 극명한 차이는 결코 우연이 아니었습니다.

- 영어 자막: Banished to the Twilight Depths
- 영어 TTS: Evolutionary history reveals that nautiloids were once far more diverse, occupying shallow marine environments throughout the Paleozoic era. However, as faster predators like ancient teleost fish and advanced marine reptiles evolved, the slower nautiloids were steadily outcompeted and pushed into marginal habitats. They found refuge in deep, dark, and nutrient-poor waters where predators were rare and competition was significantly reduced. In other words, long before the asteroid struck, nautiluses had already become experts at surviving in hostile, food-scarce conditions. This forced retreat inadvertently became their greatest evolutionary insurance policy.

- 영어 자막: The Delicate Micro-Predator
- 영어 TTS: Now let us examine what these two creatures actually ate to fuel their daily metabolic demands. Synchrotron X-ray microtomography of exceptionally preserved ammonite fossils has recently revealed their delicate mouthparts in astonishing detail. Their radula, a chitinous ribbon of tiny teeth, was exceptionally fragile and equipped with slender, comb-like projections.
