1 SECOND PER SECOND
Once you cross the
line, there is no coming back.
Here is what changes.
The moment your boots
cross that mathematical
threshold, your relationship
with the universe alters
permanently and irreversibly.
Outside the horizon,
you could always fire
an imaginary engine with
enough power to turn
around and fly back
toward Earth.
Space is open,
and you can travel
left, right, forward,
backward, or hold your
position.
But inside the event
horizon, space and time
effectively swap roles,
in a way that
defies everyday human
intuition.
Outside the horizon,
time is a one-way street.
You cannot walk backward
into yesterday, no matter
how desperately you wish
to fix a past mistake.
You are dragged inevitably
toward tomorrow at the
rate of one second per second.
Inside the event horizon,
space takes on that
exact same one-way property.
The radial direction
pointing straight toward
the center is no
longer just a direction
in physical space.
It becomes your inescapable
future.
Just as you cannot
prevent midnight from
arriving tonight, you cannot
prevent yourself from moving
toward the central
singularity.
Even if you possessed
an engine that generated
infinite thrust, firing
it would do nothing
to push you backward.
In fact, the mathematics
of general relativity
reveals a surprising result:
firing your engines inside
the horizon actually
shortens your remaining life.
Fighting the geometry only
speeds you toward the center.
What does the sky
look like now that
you are inside?
You might expect everything
to go completely black.
That is a common
misconception.
Remember, light from the
universe behind you is
still falling into the
black hole alongside you.
As you look back
over your shoulder toward
the cosmos you left
behind, you can still
see the stars shining.
However, their light is
no longer spread across
an open dome.
In simulations of such
a fall, the entire
universe outside appears
compressed into a bright,
circular window behind you.
Gravitational lensing has
squeezed the light of
billions of galaxies into
an intense, glowing iris.
As you tumble deeper,
this circular aperture
shrinks tighter and tighter,
while the darkness ahead
spreads around you like
opening jaws.
The light pouring in
from the outside universe
shifts dramatically in
color, blueshifting toward
higher, more energetic
frequencies as gravity
accelerates incoming photons
into your eyes.
Behind you, a closing window.
Ahead, only the dark.
