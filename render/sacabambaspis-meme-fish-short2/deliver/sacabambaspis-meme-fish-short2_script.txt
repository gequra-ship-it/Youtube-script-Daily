===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Imagine a fish with no jaws, no side fins, and a head wrapped in bone. That fish was real. It was called Sacabambaspis, it was about a foot long, and it lived in shallow seas around four hundred and sixty million years ago. Here is how this strange little animal was built.

Sacabambaspis belongs to a group scientists call ostracoderms, a name that means shell-skinned. These animals did not have a bony skeleton inside the body like a trout. Instead, plates of bone grew in the skin and covered the head and body like armor. Sacabambaspis was a small animal in a hard coat, and that coat is the reason we know it at all.

The front part of the fish was covered by a hard head shield, about ten centimeters long. Behind it, the body was covered with small scales, and it ended in a thin, whip-like tail. Imagine a tiny armored submarine with a flexible rear end. It was not built for speed. It was built to be hard to eat.

Sacabambaspis had no jaw. Its mouth was a narrow slit, with a lower lip made of thin strips of tiny scales. It could not bite into anything, and it could not chew. The mouth area was packed with small bony plates, nearly sixty rows of them. Scientists think these plates could move, and that they helped the fish suck food in.

How did it get around? Without side fins, the tail did the work. It probably swam by wriggling its back half from side to side, and some researchers have compared that to a tadpole. Because the head was a rigid shield, the front of the body could not bend. That would have made sharp turns and fine control difficult.

The head shield holds a quiet surprise. Its surface has tiny pores that lead into canals, and these canals were part of a lateral line system. That is the sense that lets modern fish feel movement in the water around them. For Sacabambaspis it would have been a way to detect a disturbance, such as an approaching predator, even in murky water.

The armor was made of a material called aspidine, a type of bone that has no living cells inside it. It was decorated with tiny bumps shaped like oak leaves. So even something as plain as a fish's coat of armor still has open questions. The full video on Miya's Lab shows how this fish fits into the story of animals with backbones, so watch it next.
