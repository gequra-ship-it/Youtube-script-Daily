450 MILLION YEARS AGO
Imagine a fish with
no jaw and no
side fins, locked inside
a box of bone.
This one lived,
and the fossils show how.
Sacabambaspis was a jawless
fish about twenty-five
centimeters long, and it
swam in Ordovician seas
roughly 450 million years ago.
It belongs to a
group called the jawless
fishes.
Instead of upper and
lower jaws that bite,
its mouth was a
simple opening, surrounded
by small plates.
It could not tear
flesh from prey,
and it could not
bite to defend itself.
Many scientists think it
fed by taking in
small bits of food
from the water or
the sea floor,
but we do not
have a fossil of
a meal to prove it.
Then there are the fins.
Modern fish and sharks
have paired fins on
their sides.
They work like small
wings, helping the fish
steer, roll, and brake.
Sacabambaspis had no paired
fins at all,
no pectoral fins and
no pelvic fins.
Imagine trying to steer
through open water without
rudders.
Sharp turns and hovering
in place would have
been very hard for
this animal.
So how did it move?
Almost all of its
power would have come
from its tail,
which was flattened and
ended in a fin.
By swinging its tail
from side to side,
it pushed itself forward.
The front half of
its body was covered
in bony armor,
so the head could not bend.
Scientists are still
studying exactly how well
it swam.
The armor made another
problem.
Bone is heavy.
A fish with no
swim bladder, the gas-filled
organ that many modern
fish use to float,
may have had to
keep swimming to stay
off the bottom.
Many scientists think
Sacabambaspis lived close
to the sea floor,
but this is an
interpretation based on
its body shape,
not something we can
see in the rock.
A fish like this
looks like a poor design.
But it was not alone.
Close relatives with the
same kind of armor
are known from other
parts of the world,
so this body plan worked.
Being slow and armored
was one way to
live in a sea
full of larger animals.
And it matters for us.
Our own ancestors did
not have jaws at first.
Jaws came later,
when parts of the
gill supports were reshaped
into hinged jaws.
Evolution had to start
somewhere, and it started
with fish like this.
