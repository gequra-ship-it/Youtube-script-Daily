===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Have you ever wondered what it would be like to stand on a planet where the Sun never moves across the sky? This is not science fiction. It is a reality that scientists expect for many exoplanets orbiting small red dwarf stars, and possibly for our closest interstellar neighbor, Proxima Centauri b.

When a rocky planet orbits very close to a small red dwarf star, gravitational friction can slow its spin into a state called tidal locking. This means the planet's rotation slows down until it matches its orbit. The same force keeps one face of our Moon pointed at Earth forever.

Proxima Centauri b circles its red dwarf in just eleven Earth days, from a little over four light-years away. One side of such a world could be permanently locked in blinding, blazing daylight, while the opposite side could be frozen in perpetual, pitch-black night.

On the day side, the surface could be a scorched, irradiated desert where rocks bake under an unblinking star. On the night side, temperatures plunge so low that the atmosphere itself could freeze into solid glaciers of carbon dioxide and nitrogen.

But right between these two lethal extremes, there exists a narrow, circular border called the terminator zone. Here, the star might perpetually rest on the horizon like an eternal, frozen sunset. Warm winds from the daylight hemisphere collide with freezing air from the dark side, potentially creating a stable, temperate strip where liquid water could flow across alien soil.

Scientists run computer climate models suggesting that thick atmospheres could circulate warmth across the entire globe, preventing the world from turning into a frozen tomb. Red dwarfs also erupt with violent flares, so a world like this would need a strong magnetic shield to keep its air.

Could living creatures crawl, breathe, and adapt along this perpetual twilight ring under an amber-colored sun? The deeper we peer into our cosmic neighborhood, the more we discover that nature creates habitats far stranger than our human minds ever imagined.
