2002
Imagine a creature with
no mouth, no stomach,
and a diet of solid bone.
It exists, and it
lives on the skeletons
of dead whales.
Living among these bacteria
is one of the
most bizarre animals ever
discovered: Osedax,
nicknamed the zombie worm,
or the bone-eating worm.
Researchers first found
it in 2002,
on a gray whale
skeleton in Monterey Canyon,
off California.
These worms have no
mouth and no stomach.
Instead, they have bright
red, feathery plumes that
take in oxygen from
the cold seawater.
Anchored to the skeleton,
they look like crimson
flowers blooming across
a graveyard of bones.
How can a creature
without a mouth feed
on solid bone?
The secret is its roots.
The lower part of
the female Osedax grows
into the bone and
releases acid that dissolves
it.
Inside those root-like
tissues live symbiotic
bacteria that break down
the collagen and fats
in the bone,
and the worm absorbs
the nutrients.
In a very real
sense, these worms melt
their way into the
skeleton to reach food
that has been locked
away inside.
The reproductive life of
Osedax is even stranger.
Every visible, feathery
worm is female.
So where are the males?
They are microscopic
dwarves, far smaller than
their mates, living inside
the female's gelatinous
tube, sometimes dozens
in a single tube.
They do not feed on bone.
They exist to fertilize
her eggs, so that
larvae can drift out
on the ocean currents
in search of the
next fallen giant.
Since that first discovery,
researchers have found
many different species
of Osedax, living on
whale bones in oceans
around the world.
Their larvae seem to
find bones from far
away, though how they
do it is still being studied.
Wherever a whale skeleton
rests on the seafloor,
there may be a
field of these tiny
red flowers waiting for it.
Alongside the zombie worms,
clusters of mussels,
clams, and limpets settle
on the bones.
These animals do not
filter plankton like their
shallow-water cousins.
Instead, their gills host
bacteria that use the
hydrogen sulfide seeping
from the bones.
In this pitch-black
graveyard, life runs on
chemistry.
A whole web of
animals can flourish on
the cold bones,
decades after the whale
took its last breath.
It is one of
the strangest pieces of
evidence that life finds
a way, even in a graveyard.
