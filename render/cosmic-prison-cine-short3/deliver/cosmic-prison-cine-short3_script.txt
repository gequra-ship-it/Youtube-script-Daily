===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Imagine looking up through the most powerful telescope ever built and seeing nothing. No other galaxies. No trace of the Big Bang. Just one lonely galaxy surrounded by darkness. According to physics, that is exactly what astronomers of the far future will see.

It starts with our neighborhood. The Milky Way belongs to the Local Group, a cosmic island of more than 80 known galaxies, dominated by Andromeda, the Milky Way, and the smaller Triangulum Galaxy. Within this bubble, gravity is strong enough to hold the galaxies together against the push of dark energy.

Andromeda is heading toward us at about 250,000 miles per hour. For years, astronomers predicted a collision in about 4.5 billion years, and a merger into a single giant galaxy nicknamed Milkdromeda. A 2025 study found the odds are less certain, closer to a coin flip within the next 10 billion years. But either way, the galaxies of our Local Group are expected to end up together.

Everything outside that group has a very different fate. Space is expanding, and dark energy keeps pushing distant galaxies away. More than a trillion years from now, every galaxy beyond our own merged galaxy will be carried past the cosmic horizon. Their light will be stretched until no instrument could ever detect it.

Physicists Lawrence Krauss and Robert Scherrer described what astronomers born in that era would face. They would find no sign of other galaxies racing away. The cosmic microwave background, the afterglow of the Big Bang, would be stretched beyond detection. Looking out at one galaxy surrounded by darkness, they might conclude that the universe is static and eternal. The true history of the cosmos would be erased.

Which means we are lucky. We live in a narrow window when the universe is old enough to have formed rocky planets and conscious minds, yet young enough that distant galaxies and the cosmic microwave background are still visible to our telescopes.

If humanity had appeared a hundred billion years later, the night sky would have been almost empty of other galaxies. But we live now. We are the witnesses of a disappearing masterpiece. The full story of our cosmic prison is on Miya's Lab.
