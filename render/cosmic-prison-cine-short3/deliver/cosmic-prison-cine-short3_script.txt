===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

This tiny speck of light holds every human being who has ever lived. Every king, every war, every love story, all on a point smaller than a single pixel. It is called the Pale Blue Dot, and it may be the most humbling photograph ever taken.

In 1977, NASA launched Voyager 1. It swung past Jupiter in 1979 and Saturn in 1980, then kept going, out toward the edge of the solar system, at about 38,000 miles per hour.

The astronomer Carl Sagan, a member of the Voyager imaging team, had an idea. Before the probe left for good, why not turn its camera around and take one last look back at home? Pointing the camera so close to the Sun was risky. But on February 14, 1990, the team sent the command.

From 3.7 billion miles away, Voyager 1 took 60 frames, a family portrait of the solar system. In one of them, caught inside a ray of scattered sunlight, was Earth. Just 0.12 of a pixel. A pale blue dot.

Thirty-four minutes later, Voyager 1 switched off its cameras forever, to save power for the long journey ahead. Those were the last pictures it would ever take. The signal was so faint and so far away that it took until May 1990 for all of the image data to reach Earth.

Sagan later wrote about that image in his 1994 book, Pale Blue Dot. Look again at that dot. That is here. That is home. That is us. Everyone we love, everyone we have ever heard of, lived out their lives on what he called a mote of dust suspended in a sunbeam.

Voyager 1 is still out there, now in interstellar space. On November 18, 2026, it becomes the first human-made object to reach one light-day from Earth. A radio message from us will take a full day to get there.

From out there, there are no borders and no nations. Just one small, fragile world, and the only home we have ever known. Look at it closely, and take care of it. The full story of our cosmic prison is on Miya's Lab.
