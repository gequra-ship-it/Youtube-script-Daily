===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Can we really know what color a dinosaur was? For a long time, the colors in paleoart were pure guesswork. Then, in 2010, researchers looked at fossil feathers under powerful electron microscopes and found something that changed everything.

It started in northeastern China. In 1996, a farmer in Liaoning Province found Sinosauropteryx, a small predatory dinosaur with a fringe of simple, hair-like filaments along its back and tail. It was the first non-bird dinosaur found with feather-like coverings.

The scaly, lizard-like raptors of nineties films soon looked outdated. In 2007, scientists found quill knobs on the forearm bone of a Velociraptor. In living birds, these small bumps anchor large wing feathers. So Velociraptor almost certainly had feathered arms. It was not a scaly monster, but a feathered, bird-like hunter about the size of a turkey.

In 2010, the researchers found tiny structures called melanosomes, which hold pigment. Different shapes of melanosomes give different colors in living birds. By comparing them, scientists could start to estimate the real colors of extinct animals.

Sinosauropteryx was one of the first dinosaurs to get a color. The 2010 study found that its tail had alternating bands, reddish-brown and pale, a striped tail a little like a red panda's. A later study, in 2017, suggested it was darker on top and lighter underneath, with a dark band across the eyes, like a bandit mask.

Soon other dinosaurs followed. Anchiornis, a small feathered dinosaur from China, was found to have a mostly gray and black body, white wing feathers with black tips, and a reddish crown of feathers on its head. And in 2012, a study of Microraptor found that its black feathers probably had a glossy, iridescent sheen, like a modern crow or starling.

After these discoveries, some artists put feathers on almost every dinosaur, and giant tyrannosaurs began to look like huge, fluffy birds. But in 2017, a study of skin impressions from Tyrannosaurus and its relatives found small scales on the neck, hips and tail. So adult Tyrannosaurus was probably mostly scaly.

Within a few years, dinosaurs went from pure guesswork to animals with colors backed by evidence. The full story of how pictures of extinct animals changed is on Miya's Lab.
