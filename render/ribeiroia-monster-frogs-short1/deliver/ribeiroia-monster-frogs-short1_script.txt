===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

In 1995, students in Minnesota pulled frogs out of a pond, and some of them had extra legs. Others had legs that were twisted or missing.

Many people feared pollution. Experts suspected pesticides, or stronger ultraviolet light through a thinning ozone layer.

But in California, a Stanford student named Pieter Johnson looked closely at ponds with deformed treefrogs. Around the base of their hind legs, he found tiny cysts.

Inside each one was a young parasitic flatworm called Ribeiroia ondatrae.

So his team raised clean tadpoles with the parasite. Deformities appeared in 85 percent of the frogs. Tadpoles raised without it grew normally.

The parasite gathers where back legs begin to form. One frog can end up with up to six extra legs, ten in all.

Why would a parasite build a frog like that? The full story is on Miya's Lab.
