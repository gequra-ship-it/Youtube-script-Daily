Did you know that
every breath you take
links your body to
the birth and death
of ancient stars?
You are not an
outside spectator watching
the cosmos.
You are a living,
breathing piece of the
universe itself.
Look closely at your own body.
The iron in your
blood, which helps carry
oxygen and gives blood
its red color,
was forged inside stars
and scattered by stellar
explosions billions of
years ago.
The calcium that strengthens
your bones and teeth
was made in stars
and flung across space
when they died.
The hydrogen in the
water that fills your
cells goes all the
way back to the
Big Bang, and much
of Earth's water probably
arrived on water-rich
asteroids that crashed
into the young planet.
The carbon in your
cells was forged inside
stars too, when three
helium nuclei fused together
deep in their cores.
The phosphorus in your
DNA and the sulfur
in your proteins came
from stars as well.
As the astronomer Carl
Sagan put it,
we are made of star-stuff.
When scientists look at
the elements that make
up your body,
four of them stand
out: oxygen, carbon,
hydrogen, and nitrogen.
Together they make up
about ninety-six percent
of your weight,
and they are among
the most abundant elements
in the universe.
The cosmos is not
a sterile wasteland with
one lucky planet.
Deep inside giant clouds
of gas and dust,
nature is making organic
molecules, and amino acids
have been found in
meteorites, comets, and
asteroid samples.
Many of those organic
molecules are found far
from any planet.
If the universal laws
of chemistry can arrange
ordinary cosmic stardust
into a thinking,
feeling being like you
here on Earth,
then it is very
likely that the same
chemistry is at work
on other worlds right now.
We cannot be certain yet.
But you are living
proof that the universe
can create minds.
