Masters of the sea
10,000+ LENSES
Did you know that
hundreds of millions of
years ago, creatures walked
the bottom of the
sea with eyes made
of literal rock?
It sounds like something
from a fantasy novel,
but it is real
evolutionary history.
Modern animals, including
you and me,
see through soft organic
lenses made of living cells.
When an animal dies,
those delicate tissues
break down within days.
But trilobites took a
very different approach.
Their compound eyes were
built from solid calcite
crystals, a mineral form
of calcium carbonate.
Imagine looking into ancient
waters through hundreds
of tiny stone lenses.
In some species,
there were more than
ten thousand of them,
packed side by side
across the surface of the eye.
Each one gathered light
for its own small
part of the view,
and together they built
a wide, mosaic picture
of the world around
the animal.
Some trilobites, the
phacopids, did something
even more remarkable.
Their lenses had two
layers of different
composition, a double-lens
design that scientists
have shown corrects the
blurring and distortion
that light suffers when
it passes through water.
In other words,
these animals solved an
optics problem that human
lens makers wrestled with
for centuries.
And because calcite is
a durable mineral,
the lenses did not
rot away after death.
Today, paleontologists
can slice through a
fossil trilobite eye and
study the very same
crystal lenses that once
focused light on the
floor of an ancient ocean.
We can measure their
shape, their angles,
and their optics in
detail, hundreds of millions
of years after the
last of these animals
saw anything at all.
Think about how unusual
that is.
A soft eye almost
never survives for study,
so for most ancient
animals we can only
guess what they saw.
For trilobites, we can
examine the actual lens
and ask how well
it focused light.
That is a rare
thing in science.
Most extinct animals leave
us only bones,
or shells, or footprints.
Trilobites left us their
working eyes.
So what else made
these rock-eyed creatures
masters of the sea
for hundreds of millions
of years, and what
finally ended their empire?
