1 MM
A baby about one
millimeter across, or an
egg three centimeters long.
That difference in size
may help explain why
one animal vanished and
the other survived.
Let's look at how
each one started life.
Ammonites hatched from
small eggs.
Their hatchlings were only
about one millimeter across,
with a tiny shell.
Nobody has counted ammonite
eggs, but because the
babies were so small,
scientists think that
females laid very many
of them.
The hatchlings were far
too small to swim
against ocean currents,
and the evidence suggests
that they drifted near
the surface as plankton.
In normal times,
this paid off.
Warm water and plenty
of phytoplankton let the
larvae grow quickly.
This strategy is common
among animals that live
in open water:
when the young are
cheap to make,
a few lucky survivors
are enough to keep
a species going.
But there was a
hidden weakness.
The next generation depended
on a steady supply
of tiny food floating
in the sunlight.
If that supply failed,
a whole generation could
starve.
The nautilus took the
opposite approach.
A female lays only
a small number of
large eggs, each about
three centimeters long.
Inside each egg is
a rich supply of
yolk, so the embryo
carries its own food.
The nautilus invests heavily
in each egg,
instead of making many
cheap ones.
Cold water slows
development, so a nautilus
embryo takes close to
a year to hatch.
When it finally emerges,
it is not a tiny larva.
It is a miniature
version of the adult,
about three centimeters
across, with a hard
shell, tentacles, and the
ability to swim and
search for food on its own.
Young nautiluses are thought
to stay near the
sea floor from the
start, searching for scraps
like their parents.
Then the asteroid struck,
sixty-six million years ago.
Dust and soot blocked
much of the sunlight,
and phytoplankton, the
base of the ocean
food web, suffered a
mass die-off.
Tiny ammonite larvae hatched
into an ocean with
very little plankton to eat.
Many researchers think
this may be why
every ammonite lineage
vanished.
A nautilus hatchling,
large enough to look
for scraps on the
sea floor, could live
on food that could
never feed a one-millimeter
larva.

on Miya's Lab.
