1992
A cracked egg on
your kitchen floor will
never put itself back
together.
Physics can explain why,
and it is the
real reason you cannot
go back.
The surprise is that
the basic laws of
motion do not forbid it.
Every collision involved
in breaking an egg
could, in principle,
run in reverse.
Yet no one has
ever seen yolk and
shell leap back into a hand.
The answer is a
concept called entropy,
a measure of disorder.
There are trillions of
ways for an egg
to be broken,
with the yolk and
shell spread out in a mess.
There is only one
very specific arrangement
in which the shell is whole.
Random motion almost always
lands in a messy
arrangement, simply because
there are so many
more of them.
This is the second
law of thermodynamics.
In an isolated system,
entropy tends to increase.
You see it in
an ice cube melting
in a warm drink,
a drop of ink
spreading in water,
or a neat stack
of papers sliding across
a desk.
The reverse never happens
on its own.
That steady rise of
entropy gives the universe
an arrow of time.
It is why you
remember yesterday and
not tomorrow.
It separates a past
that is fixed from
a future that is open.
Now think about time
travel to the past.
A traveler going backward
would mean reversing that
arrow, turning broken eggs
into whole ones across
a whole region of
the universe.
The odds against this
are astronomical, and no
known process does it.
Stephen Hawking made a
different argument.
In 1992, he proposed
the Chronology Protection
Conjecture, that the laws
of physics prevent time
machines from forming at all.
His argument came from
quantum physics rather
than thermodynamics.
It is still a
conjecture and not a
proven fact.
Two different lines of
thinking, one from entropy
and one from quantum
physics, point the same way.
But we should be honest.
Entropy explains why the
arrow of time exists.
It does not by
itself prove that a
loop in time is impossible.
What it does give
us is a feeling
for time's weight.
You cannot redo a
clumsy sentence or relive
a happy evening.
Every choice carries meaning
because you cannot take
it back, and every
conversation with a friend
is precious because that
exact moment will never
come again.
