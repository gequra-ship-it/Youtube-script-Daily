1990S
2007
If this parasite is
old, why did the
1990s bring such a
startling wave of deformed
frogs?
This is where humans
re-enter the story.
Pieter Johnson and his
colleagues found a link
between nutrient pollution
and the severity of
these infections.
Farmers use large amounts
of fertilizer containing
nitrogen and phosphorus
to boost crop yields.
When heavy rain washes
the surplus into nearby
wetlands, it sets off
a chain reaction.
First, the nutrients feed
blooms of green algae
that blanket the pond
in thick green sludge.
For ramshorn snails,
that algae is an
all-you-can-eat buffet.
Snail populations in
nutrient-rich ponds can
grow sharply.
More snails means more
homes for Ribeiroia.
Ordinary ponds can turn
into parasite-breeding
superfactories.
A study published in
2007 found that nutrient
enrichment raised snail
numbers, and those snails
then released more
infectious parasites into
the water.
In a balanced pond,
a tadpole might meet
only a few cercariae,
and the effects may be minor.
In a nutrient-rich pond,
a tadpole can be
attacked by many parasites
at once.
Its developing body is
overwhelmed by the sheer
number of invaders.
What was once a
rare curiosity can become
an epidemic of monsters.
It is worth being
honest about the limits
of this story.
Ribeiroia explains the
multi-legged frogs, but
it is not the
only threat amphibians face.
Habitat loss, disease,
and other factors also
harm frogs around the
world, and not every
deformity in every pond
has the same cause.
Nature rarely offers a
single villain.
Think of how many
links had to line
up: rain, fertilizer,
algae, snails, parasites,
tadpoles, birds.
Push too hard on
one link and the
whole pond can tip over.
It also shows why
the first answer,
a toxic spill,
felt so natural.
People look for a
single villain.
What looked like a
simple story of toxic
dumping turned out to
be an intricate chain
linking farmers, fertilizer,
algae, snails, flatworms,
tadpoles, and birds.
Every small change we
introduce ripples outward
in ways we cannot predict.
Next time you stand
beside a quiet pond,
remember that its glassy
surface hides an invisible
battlefield.
