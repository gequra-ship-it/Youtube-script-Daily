By day, this beetle
shares a shelter with
frogs and toads.
At night, it hunts them.
It is an adult
Epomis ground beetle,
metallic blue or green,
with a yellow-orange rim.
And unlike most beetles,
it attacks amphibians.
It jumps onto a
frog's back and hangs
on like a rodeo rider.
The frog's tongue can
only strike forward,
so it cannot reach it.
Then the beetle makes
a horizontal cut across
the lower back.
Within one to two
minutes, the frog stops
moving.
Within a few hours,
only the head and
limbs remain.
Even green toads,
with poison glands in
their skin, are on the menu.
How the beetle copes
with the poison,
no one knows for sure.
And its larva has
an even stranger trick.
