What if the reason
we have not met
an advanced alien
civilization is not that
they are hiding,
but that they have
not been born yet?
It sounds shocking,
but some astrophysicists
take this possibility
seriously.
When the Big Bang
happened about thirteen
point eight billion years
ago, the young universe
contained almost nothing
but hydrogen and helium.
There was no carbon
for organic molecules,
very little oxygen for
water, and no iron
for the cores of
rocky planets.
The first generations of
stars had to live
their lives and explode
as supernovas to forge
and scatter heavier elements
across the cosmos.
The most massive of
those early stars burned
out quickly, but it
took many generations of
stars, and a very
long time, to build up enough.
Over billions of years,
that slow cosmic alchemy
built up the raw
materials that rocky planets
and living things need.
Earth formed about four
and a half billion
years ago, when our
galaxy had finally collected
enough of those heavier
elements.
And the universe is
far from finished.
Stars could keep forming
for trillions of years
to come.
That means we might
not be late to
a crowded cosmic party.
We might be the
early birds, arriving just
as the lights come
on and the music
begins to play.
Think about how recent we are.
Our sun formed only
about four and a
half billion years ago,
roughly nine billion years
after the Big Bang,
so there was plenty
of time for earlier
worlds, but also plenty
of time still to come.
This is a hypothesis,
not a fact,
and no one knows for sure.
But if it is
right, then we could
be part of the
first wave of intelligent
life to open its
eyes and look at
the night sky.
Our civilization might
not be a lonely anomaly.
It might be the
opening chapter of a
story that will last
for an unimaginably long time.
