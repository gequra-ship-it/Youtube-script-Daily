===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Why would a tiny worm want a frog to be eaten?

The parasite Ribeiroia ondatrae needs three hosts: a snail, then a tadpole, and finally a bird, such as a heron.

Inside a ram's horn snail, it multiplies and castrates its host. One infected snail can release more than 1,000 larvae in a single night.

The larvae burrow into tadpoles right where the back legs are forming. Some frogs grow extra legs. Others lose them.

In one study, malformed frogs jumped 41 percent shorter distances swam 37 percent slower, and caught 55 percent fewer crickets.

Scientists have proposed that this makes them easier prey. When a heron eats one, the parasite grows up inside the bird, and its eggs return to the water.

And fertilizer runoff can feed the snails, making the cycle stronger. The full story is on Miya's Lab.
