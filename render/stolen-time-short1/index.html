You walk through a
shallow alien ocean for
three hours.
When you climb back
to your ship,
twenty-three years have
passed on Earth.
This is real physics.
Gravity slows time.
Clocks deep in a
gravitational well run
slower than clocks far away.
GPS satellites prove it
every day, gaining about
forty-five microseconds
from gravity alone.
To stretch three hours
into twenty-three years,
you need a supermassive
black hole of about
one hundred million Suns,
spinning at almost its
maximum rate.
The planet must orbit
right at the innermost
stable orbit, racing at
roughly half the speed
of light, just above
the point of no return.
Even the skyscraper-high
waves are physics:
tidal bulges raised by
the black hole,
with the planet rocking
underneath them.
Physicist Kip Thorne worked
out every number.
The full story of
stolen time is on Miya's Lab.
