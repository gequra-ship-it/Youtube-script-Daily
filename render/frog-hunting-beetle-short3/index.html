3 STAGES
UP TO 9
What do you do
when a tiny creature
is locked onto you
and you cannot shake it off?
For a young frog
in the Epomis experiments,
there was no good answer.
Once the larva has
bitten on, the frog panics.
It shakes its head.
It rubs at its skin.
But the larva is
small, and its curved
jaws hold firm.
The harder the frog
struggles, the clearer
it becomes that its
best weapon, the tongue,
cannot help here.
Now the larva begins to feed.
It cannot swallow chunks
of meat.
Like many ground beetle
larvae, it works in
a different way.
First it sucks out
the body fluids of
its victim, and later
it chews.
Ground beetle larvae often
use digestive fluids to
soften their food before
they take it in.
That lets a tiny
animal eat something far
larger than its own mouth.
The press reports on
the study describe a
slow process.
In the end,
the researchers report
that all that remains
of the amphibian is
a pile of bones.
Here is the part
that is easy to get wrong.
Some popular stories
describe a paralyzing venom
in the larva.
We have not seen
a clear measurement in
the original study to
support that, so we
will not claim it.
What we can say
is what the researchers
saw: the larva stays
attached, it feeds,
and the amphibian dies.
And this is only
the first of three stages.
At the end of
each stage, the larva
drops off its host
and molts in a hidden place.
Each new stage needs
a new meal.
Reports on the study
say that a larva
kills just one amphibian
in its first stage,
and as many as
nine in its third.
These numbers come from
news summaries of the
research, so treat them
as a rough picture,
not a precise count.
Imagine something far
smaller than its victims
taking down several animals
over a short life.
It does not use speed.
It does not use size.
It uses a disguise,
a good dodge,
and a very strong grip.
After the third stage,
the larva digs into
the soil and builds
a small chamber.
Inside, in the dark,
its body is taken
apart and rebuilt.
Weeks later, an adult
beetle comes out.
This is one of
the strangest role reversals
in the animal kingdom.
