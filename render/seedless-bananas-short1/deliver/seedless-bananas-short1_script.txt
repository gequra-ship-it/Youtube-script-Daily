===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Cut open a banana, and you will find tiny dark specks but no seeds that can grow. So how does a fruit with no working seeds fill supermarkets across the world? The answer begins with a rare accident in the plant's chromosomes.

Wild bananas are packed with many hard, black seeds, each a few millimeters across, and very little soft flesh. Eating one is mostly a chore of picking out seeds. That was the normal pattern for wild plants in the genus Musa for millions of years.

Wild bananas are diploid, with two sets of chromosomes. Scientists think a rare irregular cell division in a hybrid plant produced an egg or pollen grain with two sets instead of one. When it joined a normal one, the offspring had three sets. A plant with three sets is called a triploid.

When a triploid banana tries to make eggs and pollen, its chromosomes cannot pair up evenly, because three cannot be split into two equal groups. The cells get mixed up and cannot form working seeds, so the plant is nearly sterile. In the wild, that would usually be a dead end.

But bananas have another trick, called parthenocarpy. The fruit can swell and fill with soft pulp without pollination or fertilization. Instead of a mouthful of hard seeds, there was a soft, sweet, seedless fruit. Rare plants like this appeared, and people noticed. The earliest evidence of banana farming, from New Guinea, is about seven thousand years old.

So how do you plant a banana with no seeds? You do not need seeds. The banana is not a tree but a giant herb, and underground it has a thick stem called a corm. From the corm, small shoots called suckers keep growing, and they are genetic copies of the parent. Farmers cut a sucker away and plant it, and within roughly a year, it can bear fruit.

Today, many farms use tissue culture. Scientists take a tiny piece of tissue from the growing tip of a healthy plant, clean it, and grow it on sterile jelly with nutrients. From one small piece, thousands of identical young plants can be grown.

So bananas do not spread across continents on their own. They depend on people, who have cloned them for thousands of years. And that creates a risk we explore in the full video. Watch the full deep dive on Miya's Lab to discover the secret of Big Mike, the banana the world largely lost.
