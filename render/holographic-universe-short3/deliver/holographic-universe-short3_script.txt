===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

You may have seen headlines saying that scientists proved the universe is a hologram. They did not. So what do we actually know? Let us sort the facts onto four shelves, from solid to open.

First shelf, well supported by theory. A black hole's entropy is proportional to the area of its horizon, not the volume inside, and quantum theory demands that information is preserved.

Second shelf, predicted but never observed from a real black hole: Hawking radiation. Experiments with analogue systems, such as sound waves in flowing fluids, mimic the effect, but those are not real black holes.

Third shelf, a conjecture with strong evidence. In nineteen ninety-seven, Juan Maldacena proposed that gravity in anti-de Sitter space is equivalent to a quantum theory on its boundary. It has passed an enormous number of checks, but it is not proven in the strict mathematical sense.

Here is the catch. Anti-de Sitter space has a negative cosmological constant and a boundary like the edge of a box. Measurements of distant exploding stars in the late nineteen nineties showed that our universe is accelerating, which fits a small positive cosmological constant. Holography for a universe like ours is far less understood.

Fourth shelf, the open questions. Our universe also has a cosmological horizon, and in nineteen seventy-seven Gary Gibbons and Stephen Hawking showed that such a horizon has a temperature and an entropy proportional to its area. That is a hint. But nobody has yet found the dictionary for a universe like ours.

Direct tests are hard. Craig Hogan proposed that a holographic graininess of space might show up as a tiny jitter. In twenty fifteen, the Holometer at Fermilab did not see the jitter that his particular model predicts, and that ruled the model out. It does not disprove the holographic principle as most physicists state it.

So the honest answer is a powerful idea, real mathematics, and no proof for our universe. Watch the full video on Miya's Lab to see how the story unfolds.
