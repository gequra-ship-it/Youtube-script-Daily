Now for the third
secret hidden within your
banana, a fact that
sounds like urban legend
but is verified by
physicists: the banana
you eat is naturally
radioactive.
In fact, bananas are
so reliably radioactive
that physicists literally
created an informal unit
of nuclear radiation
measurement called the
"Banana Equivalent Dose."
Why is a common
fruit radioactive?
The answer is potassium,
an essential mineral that
bananas contain in high
concentration.
But in nature,
roughly zero point zero
one two percent of
all naturally occurring
potassium exists in the
form of Potassium-40,
an unstable, radioactive
isotope that undergoes
spontaneous nuclear decay.
As Potassium-40 decays,
it emits beta particles
and high-energy gamma rays.
This means that every
single banana generates
a tiny, measurable burst
of ionizing radiation.
Shipping containers full
of bananas have even
triggered radiation
detectors at ports and
borders, setting off false
alarms until agents realize
the cargo is merely fruit!
Before you panic,
consider the scale.
A single banana exposes
you to approximately zero
point one microsieverts
of radiation.
To put that into
perspective, you would
need to eat tens
of millions of bananas
in a single sitting
to receive a lethal
dose of radiation,
which is physically
impossible.
Your body also keeps
its potassium level in
balance, filtering out
any excess through the
kidneys.
Eating a banana is
completely safe.
The banana has one
more identity crisis:
botanically speaking, a
banana is a berry,
but a strawberry is not!
In botany, a true
berry is defined very
specifically as a fleshy
fruit produced from a
flower with a single
ovary, containing three
distinct layers: an outer
exocarp skin, a fleshy
mesocarp, and an inner
endocarp encasing the seeds.
The banana satisfies every
single scientific
requirement, while
strawberries and raspberries
fail completely.
A banana is a
radioactive berry, and
that is just one
of its strange secrets.
