===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

If nothing can travel faster than light, could we simply cheat? Science fiction has two favorite shortcuts: wormholes and warp drives. Physicists have taken both seriously. And both run into walls that may never be broken.

First, the rule they are trying to dodge. Light travels at about 186,000 miles per second. It is the universal speed limit of cause and effect, and nothing with mass can ever exceed it, or even reach it. As an object speeds up toward light, every extra bit of speed costs more energy than the last, and the total climbs toward infinity.

So what about wormholes? These Einstein-Rosen bridges could, in theory, connect distant regions of spacetime, like a tunnel through the fabric of the universe. But to hold a wormhole open long enough for anything to pass, you would need exotic matter with negative energy, in amounts no one knows how to make. It may not exist at all.

And even then, quantum physics may add another hurdle. Stephen Hawking proposed that quantum fluctuations could build up inside such a tunnel and destroy it before it could ever be used for time travel. He called this the chronology protection conjecture. No one knows for sure, because we still lack a complete theory of quantum gravity.

Then there is the warp drive. In 1994, physicist Miguel Alcubierre showed mathematically that a spacecraft could, in theory, travel faster than light by contracting space in front of it and expanding space behind it. Inside the warp bubble, the ship itself is at rest, so it never breaks the local speed limit.

The catch? Early calculations said a warp bubble would need at least the mass-energy of the planet Jupiter, and it would have to be negative energy. Later redesigns lowered that number, but no one knows how to make negative energy in useful amounts.

Worse still, a 2012 study found that a moving warp bubble could sweep up particles along the way, like a cosmic snowplow. When the ship stopped, that trapped energy could burst out ahead of it, blasting anything waiting at the destination.

Every escape route runs into the same wall. The laws of nature are not designed for interstellar nomads. To see why we are trapped inside our cosmic neighborhood, watch the full story on Miya's Lab.
