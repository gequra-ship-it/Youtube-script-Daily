As if a missing
27 percent were not
baffling enough, the
universe had an even
bigger surprise waiting
for us.
For decades, scientists
assumed that gravity would
gradually slow down the
expansion of the universe
that began with the Big Bang.
It seemed natural.
Throw a ball upward,
and gravity slows it down.
The pull of all
the matter in the
universe should slow the
expansion in the same way.
But when researchers
observed distant exploding
stars across cosmic time,
they discovered the exact
opposite.
The expansion of our
universe is not slowing down.
It is speeding up.
Something seems to be
pushing space itself apart,
overcoming the mutual
gravitational pull of all
the galaxies.
Imagine tossing a ball
into the air and
watching it rise faster
and faster instead of
slowing and falling.
You would immediately
suspect something unseen
was pushing it.
That is how astronomers
felt when they looked
at those distant explosions.
To explain this relentless
acceleration, scientists
introduced the concept
of dark energy.
It makes up roughly
68 percent of everything
that exists.
Unlike matter, which clumps
together into stars and
planets, dark energy appears
to be smoothly woven
into the very structure
of empty space itself.
In the simplest model,
as the universe expands
and creates more empty
space, more of this
mysterious energy appears
with it.
It represents the dominant
ingredient of our universe,
yet its true nature
remains one of the
deepest mysteries in all
of science.
Notice how different this
feels from dark matter.
Dark matter acts like a glue.
It gathers around galaxies
and pulls inward.
Dark energy does not
gather anywhere.
It pushes outward across
the whole universe,
and it grows more
important as space grows
larger.
One holds things together.
The other pushes everything
apart.
The name dark energy
is a label for
whatever is responsible,
a description of how
it behaves, not an
answer to what it is.
