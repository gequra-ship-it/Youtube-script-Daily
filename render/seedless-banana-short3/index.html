Where did the replacement
come from?
From the private greenhouse
of an English aristocrat.
The salvation of the
banana industry came from
the most unlikely place
imaginable: the private
greenhouse of an English
aristocrat.
In the 1830s,
William Cavendish, the
sixth Duke of Devonshire,
grew a banana plant
at his Chatsworth estate,
one that had reached
England by way of
Mauritius and originally
came from Chinese stock.
Decades later, growers
discovered that this
variety, named the
"Cavendish" after him,
resisted the deadly Tropical
Race 1 fungus.
At first, the Cavendish
was considered inferior:
bland, less fragrant,
and easily bruised.
Shipping it required boxes,
careful packing, and
refrigerated containers
at a massive financial cost.
But the industry had
little choice: it was
the Cavendish, or no
bananas at all.
This brings us directly
to the second and
most alarming secret of
all: history is repeating
itself right now,
and our current banana
is living on borrowed time.
Having learned little from
the Gros Michel,
the global agricultural
machine replaced one massive
clone monoculture with
another.
Today, the Cavendish
accounts for the
overwhelming majority of
exported bananas.
We built the exact
same house of cards,
and the wind has
begun to blow.
A different strain of
the same soil fungus,
Tropical Race 4,
or TR4, was first
identified in Taiwan and
has since spread across
Asia, Australia, the Middle
East, and Africa.
Unlike the strain that
wiped out the Gros
Michel, TR4 can kill
the Cavendish.
It attacks through the
roots, clogs the vascular
pipelines, cuts off water
supplies, and rots the
plant from the inside out.
Once TR4 enters the
soil, that soil is
cursed for decades.
Then, in August 2019,
the ultimate nightmare
scenario finally occurred:
TR4 was officially detected
in Colombia, the
agricultural heartland
of South American banana
production.
The Colombian government
declared a national
emergency and moved to
quarantine the affected
farms and disinfect vehicles
entering the zone.
Peru later reported
infections too, proving
that the wall had
been breached.
The clock may already
be ticking for the Cavendish.
History is repeating itself.
