A DAY OR TWO
Meet the strangest traveler
in the pond.
It is a flatworm,
it has no brain,
and it needs three
different kinds of animals
to finish its life.
Think about how strange
that is.
A single brainless worm
needs a snail,
an amphibian, and a
bird, all in the
right place at the
right time, just to
finish one lap of its life.
Step one is the
ramshorn snail.
A microscopic swimming
larva called a miracidium
burrows into the snail's
soft tissue.
Once inside, it behaves
like an invading army.
It feeds on the
snail's reproductive tissue
and sterilizes the snail,
diverting the snail's energy
into feeding the parasite.
Inside this living factory,
the flatworm multiplies
into huge numbers of clones.
Step two begins after
some weeks, when the
clones mature into a
free-swimming stage called
cercariae.
A single infected snail
can release large numbers
of these fork-tailed
swimmers into the water.
They have no mouths,
and their energy reserves
are limited.
They are generally thought
to survive only a
day or two,
so they have one
desperate mission: find
the second host.
That host is a
developing tadpole.
At this delicate stage,
small clusters of cells
called limb buds are
forming near the base
of its tail.
They hold the instructions
for growing two matched
hind legs.
And the cercariae head
straight for that spot.
On contact, the parasite
attacks the skin.
It sheds its swimming
tail, pushes its small
body through the tissue,
and burrows toward the
limb bud.
Once settled deep inside
the limb-forming area,
it builds a thick
wall around itself.
It becomes an immobile,
armored cyst called a
metacercaria.
These cysts disturb the
growing tissue, and the
chemical signals that guide
limb growth, including
a vitamin A derivative
called retinoic acid,
are thrown off.
Scientists are still working
out exactly how.
The result can be
a scrambled building plan.
Instead of making one
leg on each side,
the tissue can start
the process more than
once, and extra,
fully formed legs may
emerge from the same hip.
But where is the third host?
To find out,
look up at the sky.
