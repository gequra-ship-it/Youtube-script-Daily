Can the wind dig
a hole deep enough
to find water?
In parts of the
desert, it did.
And that is only
one of the ways
water finds its way
up through dry ground.
One way is pressure.
In an artesian aquifer,
the weight of the
water upslope does the
pushing.
If a crack or
a fault breaks through
the rock above it,
water can rise to
the surface on its
own, with no pump at all.
Wind can do it too.
In open desert,
strong winds blow loose
sand and dust away
for thousands of years.
Where the rock is
weak, especially where
salt crystals break it
apart, the ground is
lowered bit by bit.
Below every desert there
is a level called
the water table.
If wind lowers the
ground far enough,
the bottom of the
hollow reaches that level,
and water seeps out.
The wind has dug
down to the water.
The most dramatic example
is the Qattara Depression
in northwestern Egypt.
Its lowest point is
about one hundred
thirty-three meters below
sea level.
Scientists think wind,
together with salt
weathering, removed the
rock that once filled it.
Its floor is salt
marsh and salt flat.
Faults can create oases too.
A fault is a
break in the Earth's
crust where rock has moved.
Along some faults,
ground-up rock and clay
form a barrier,
like an underground dam.
Groundwater is blocked
and pushed upward,
and a line of
springs can appear.
You can see this
in California's Coachella
Valley.
Desert fan palms,
the only palm native
to the western United
States, grow in groves
right along the San
Andreas Fault.
Crushed rock in the
fault zone brings
groundwater near the surface.
A famous earthquake fault
keeps these palms alive.
Even the driest deserts
get short, violent storms.
Hard ground cannot soak
up the rain,
so it rushes down
dry valleys called wadis
as flash floods.
But part of the
water sinks into the
sand and gravel of
the valley floor.
Long after the surface
dries, water can remain below.
Where it comes close
to the surface,
wells can reach it,
and palms and gardens
can grow.
Wind, faults and floods:
three different ways to
bring hidden water up.

how oases are made
