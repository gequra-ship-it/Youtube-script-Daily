Thanks, fault!
In California's desert,
a line of palm
trees grows right on
top of the San Andreas Fault.
That is not a coincidence.
A fault is a
break in the Earth's
crust where rock has moved.
Along it, rock is
crushed into fine,
clay-rich material.
That crushed rock blocks
groundwater like an
underground dam.
Water flowing toward the
fault is pushed up
toward the surface.
Where it comes out,
desert fan palms grow,
the only palm native
to the western United States.
They need water all
year, so their groves
trace exactly where it
reaches the surface.
Thousand Palms Oasis in
the Coachella Valley is
one of these groves.
One of the most
famous earthquake faults
on Earth also keeps
these palms alive.
Faults are just one
way oases are made.

on Miya's Lab.
