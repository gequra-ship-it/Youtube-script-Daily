===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Did you know that every star, planet, and galaxy you can see in the night sky makes up just under five percent of the cosmos? Everything humanity has ever studied, from the smallest atom to the most colossal galaxy, accounts for only about five percent.

Here is an even stranger twist. Even inside that five percent, the stars we love to photograph are only a sliver. When astronomers add up all the starlight in the cosmos, they find that stars hold well under one percent of the total. Most ordinary matter is thin, hot gas, far too faint to see with the naked eye.

Imagine the universe as a puzzle of one hundred pieces. Only five of them are made of the ordinary matter that builds planets, stars, and you. The other ninety-five pieces are invisible. Scientists call them dark matter and dark energy.

Dark matter accounts for roughly twenty-seven percent of the entire universe, dwarfing everything we know by more than five times. It is not made of protons, neutrons, or electrons. It emits no light, absorbs no light, and reflects no light, yet scientists suspect its gravity is the glue that holds galaxies together.

Dark energy makes up roughly sixty-eight percent. In the 1990s, astronomers studying distant exploding stars discovered that the expansion of our universe is not slowing down. It is speeding up. Something is actively pushing the fabric of spacetime apart, and we call it dark energy.

If a cloud of dark matter passed through your living room right now, you would not see it, hear it, or feel it touch your skin. Leading hypotheses suggest it may consist of undiscovered subatomic particles that barely interact with normal matter at all, but no one knows for sure.

Normal matter makes up roughly five percent. Dark matter claims about twenty-seven percent. And dark energy commands an overwhelming sixty-eight percent. Together, dark matter and dark energy make up roughly ninety-five to ninety-six percent of the total cosmic pie. It is a humbling picture of our reality.

When you step outside tonight and look up at the night sky, remember that the stars and galaxies are not the main show. They are merely tiny specks of bright foam floating on top of a vast, unseen ocean. To explore the quiet frontiers of this mystery, watch the full video on Miya's Lab, and let us illuminate the unknown together.
