===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Most of the galaxies you can see through a telescope are already out of reach. Not for now. Forever. Even a ship traveling at the speed of light could never get there. And the reason is one of the strangest facts in all of physics.

Start with the speed limit. Light travels at about 186,000 miles per second, and according to Einstein, it is the universal speed limit of cause and effect. Nothing with mass can ever exceed it, or even reach it. That alone makes the universe enormous for us. But it is not what seals us in.

What seals us in is the expansion of space. Galaxies are not really flying through space like bullets. Instead, the fabric of space itself is stretching between them. Between you and a distant galaxy, new cubic light-years of empty space keep appearing, every single moment. And Hubble's Law tells us that the speed at which a galaxy recedes is proportional to its distance. Twice as far away, twice as fast.

Now connect the dots. At a distance of about 14 billion light-years from Earth, the space between us and those regions is growing faster than light. Does that break Einstein's rule? No. Relativity says nothing can move through space faster than light. It says nothing about how fast space itself can stretch.

Imagine running on a giant treadmill. Your top speed is 10 miles per hour, but the belt beneath you moves backward at 20, then 50, then 100 miles per hour. No matter how hard you run, you are dragged backward. For a beam of light trying to reach the most distant galaxies, the cosmic treadmill moves faster than its top speed.

The true point of no return lies at roughly 16 billion light-years. It is called the cosmic event horizon. Any galaxy beyond it is cut off from us for good. A beam of light fired toward those regions right now would travel forever and never arrive.

And here is the part that hurts. By one widely cited estimate, even a ship launched at the speed of light today could never reach about 94 percent of the galaxies in the observable universe. We can look at them. We can photograph them and study their past. But we can never touch them.

So where is the real edge of the universe, and why are we trapped inside it? The full story is on Miya's Lab.
