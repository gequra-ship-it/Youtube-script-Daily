Sit down for one
hour on this planet,
and about seven point
six years pass on Earth.
Your kids learn to
talk, start school,
and grow up without you.
Gravity near a supermassive
black hole slows time,
and it only works one way.
You can jump into
the future, but you
can never come back.
Even a radio message
from the surface is stretched.
A half-second hello takes
many hours to reach
the ship above.
Meanwhile, the crewmate
waiting in orbit watches
your lander crawl like
a fly trapped in
amber, for more than
eight thousand four hundred
days.
When you return after
three hours, the airlock
opens on a face
with gray hair and
twenty-three years of
loneliness.
Physics does not need malice.
It only needs mass.
The full story is
on Miya's Lab.
