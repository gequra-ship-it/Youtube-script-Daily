===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Your phone's map only works because Einstein was right. How can a clock in space tick faster than a clock on the ground, and what would happen to your directions if engineers ignored it?

For hundreds of years, classical mechanics reigned over human scientific thought, thanks to Sir Isaac Newton. Newton believed the universe ran on one absolute master clock, where a second in London was identical to a second on the distant surface of Mars.

That tidy universe came crashing down in the early twentieth century, when an obscure patent clerk in Switzerland named Albert Einstein looked deeper into the nature of light. He realized that the speed of light in a vacuum is an absolute cosmic speed limit, about 299,792 kilometers per second, and that it never changes. If the speed of light must be the same for everyone, then distance and time themselves must bend to make it so.

Ten years later, in 1915, Einstein took this further with his theory of general relativity. Space and time are woven into a single, four-dimensional fabric known as spacetime. Instead, gravity is the geometric warping of this fabric, caused by mass and energy.

This warping leads to gravitational time dilation. When a massive object curves spacetime, it stretches not just the dimensions of space, but the rate at which time passes. Clocks sitting deeper within a gravitational well run measurably slower than clocks resting farther away, in weaker gravity.

You do not need to voyage to the edge of the galaxy to prove that this is real. The GPS satellites orbiting thousands of kilometers above our planet sit in weaker gravity than your phone on the ground. Because they experience less spacetime curvature, the atomic clocks aboard those satellites run about forty-five microseconds faster every day from gravity alone. If engineers did not correct for Einstein's equations, satellite navigation would drift by kilometers every single day.

On Earth, the difference in time flow is measured in millionths of a second, because our planet is a tiny cosmic pebble with modest gravity. Near a supermassive black hole, time dilation scales up from imperceptible microseconds to entire human lifetimes.
