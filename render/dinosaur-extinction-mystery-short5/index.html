Who could endure a
planet stripped of light
and warmth?
In this dark and
broken world, being large
and powerful was no
longer an advantage.
The survivors were not
the giant monsters with
razor-sharp teeth, but
modest creatures.
Small mammals that could
burrow underground, animals
that could feed on
dead plants and animals,
and small birds managed
to scrape by.
When the sunlight finally
returned, the reign of
the non-avian dinosaurs
was permanently over.
Survival was not about
strength.
It was about being
small, about being able
to hide, and about
being able to live
on whatever was left.
A creature that could
burrow underground could
wait out the worst
of the heat and the cold.
Now look at the word birds.
Birds are the living
branch of the feathered
dinosaurs.
When the giants disappeared,
small birds were among
those who endured.
Remember the sparrow from
the beginning of this story.
That is why a
living dinosaur can still
land on a branch
outside your window.
So the next time
you see a small
bird hop across the
ground, remember what it
carries in its ancestry.
The great reptiles were
not erased from the
world entirely.
Their smallest relatives
endured the dark years,
and they are still here.
Today, as you drink
your morning coffee or
walk along a crowded
city sidewalk, you are
standing on the other
side of that ancient event.
When the reign of
the great reptiles ended,
the world opened up
for small mammals that
had been living in
their shadow, and their
descendants include us.
A stray asteroid may
be part of the
reason we are here
to ask about it.
The story of the
dinosaurs is not merely
an ancient tragedy.
It is also a
testament to the resilience
of life.
Even beneath a blanket
of ash and darkened
skies, life endured,
and it slowly rebuilt
a living world.
It is a story
of loss, but it
is also a story
of how life finds
a way through the
worst day it has ever had.
A thin line of
clay, a buried circle
under the sea,
and a sparrow on
a branch are all
pieces of the same
long answer.
