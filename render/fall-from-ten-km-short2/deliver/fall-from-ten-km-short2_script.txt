===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

If you were falling from the sky without a parachute, would you aim for a deep lake or a dense forest? Most people instinctively choose the water, picturing a deep, forgiving dive. But the physics points the other way.

A person falling belly-down through the lower atmosphere settles at roughly two hundred kilometers per hour, about fifty-five meters per second. At that speed, the surface you meet decides almost everything.

Water is almost incompressible, and it cannot move out of the way fast enough when you strike it at that speed. Instead of letting you in gently, it pushes back hard, much like a solid surface. Hitting a lake or the ocean flat-on is very often fatal.

The secret variable is stopping distance. When a body stops over a few millimeters, the force is enormous. Lengthen the distance, and the time, and the peak force falls. As an illustration, stopping over three meters instead of three centimeters spreads the same energy across a hundred times more distance.

That is why dense trees matter. As a body hits the top of the canopy, thin twigs snap first and take a modest share of the energy. Then thicker branches bend and break, absorbing much more. Many small collisions stretch the stop across several meters instead of a split second.

Deep, freshly fallen snow can do something similar. It is mostly trapped air in a lattice of crystals, so it compresses gradually, like the crumple zone of a car. On a steep slope, a head-on impact becomes a glancing blow, and the body slides, shedding speed across distance.

Real survivors show the pattern. In 1972, flight attendant Vesna Vulovic fell about ten thousand meters with part of her aircraft, and accounts say she came down on a wooded, snowy slope. In 1971, Juliane Koepcke fell about three kilometers, still strapped into her seat, into the Peruvian rainforest.

Stretch an impact from one hundredth of a second to one tenth of a second, and the average force drops to one tenth. That is the difference between a stop that breaks a body and one that, with a lot of luck, it can survive.
