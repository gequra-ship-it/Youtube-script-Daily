In this fight,
a frog meets a
beetle larva about two
centimeters long.
And the frog is
the one that gets eaten.
The larva belongs to
a ground beetle called Epomis.
It feeds only on
amphibians, animals much
bigger than itself.
It sits still and
waves its antennae and jaws.
To a hungry frog,
that looks like a
small, wriggling meal.
The closer the frog
comes, the faster the
larva waves.
The frog strikes.
Its tongue flicks out
in about a tenth
of a second.
But the larva dodges,
and it latches on
with double-hooked jaws.
In 382 lab trials,
the amphibian almost always
lost.
One toad even swallowed
a larva, spat it
out alive two hours
later, and was then
eaten by it.
The full story,
including what the adult
beetle does at night,
is on Miya's Lab.
