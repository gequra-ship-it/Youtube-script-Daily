===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Imagine looking up at the sky tonight, and instead of seeing our tiny silver Moon, you see massive alien worlds hanging above your head like giant glowing globes. This breathtaking sky may be real in a planetary system located forty light-years away called TRAPPIST-1.

At the center of this system sits a miniature, ultra-cool red dwarf star barely larger than Jupiter. Yet, circling this tiny star is the most incredible planetary family ever discovered: seven rocky, Earth-sized worlds packed into an astonishingly tight formation. It is the largest cluster of Earth-sized worlds ever found circling a single star.

All seven planets orbit closer to their host star than Mercury orbits our Sun. Because they are crowded so close together, if you stood on the surface of TRAPPIST-1e, your neighboring sibling worlds would look larger in your sky than the full Moon looks from Earth.

With your bare eyes, you might glimpse their swirling cloud formations, mountain ranges, and glittering ice sheets drift across the twilight sky. Even more mind-blowing, three of these seven worlds sit squarely within the star's Goldilocks zone, where liquid oceans could pool on their surfaces.

Among them, TRAPPIST-1e has a size, mass, and gravitational pull in the same range as Earth's, making it one of the most promising candidates found so far for alien life. Theoretical climate models suggest it might possess a dense iron core capable of generating a protective magnetic field.

If it managed to retain its water and atmosphere through its star's turbulent youth, it could host vast oceans. Right now, the James Webb Space Telescope is aiming its massive golden mirrors at this tiny system, analyzing the sliver of starlight filtering through their atmospheres to hunt for oxygen, water vapor, and methane.

When a planet passes in front of its star, a sliver of starlight filters through its atmosphere, and the missing wavelengths reveal which gases are there. We are closer than ever to discovering whether those majestic skies harbor oceans, rain, and living cells.
