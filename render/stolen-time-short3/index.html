Could an ocean planet
orbit a black hole
and survive?
Physicists say there is
a way, but only just.
This is a theoretical
world, and no one
has found one.
But this creates a
deadly problem.
A planet cannot survive
near an ordinary,
non-rotating black hole.
Close to the horizon,
no stable circular orbit
exists, so a planet
parked there would lose
its balance and fall in.
The extreme time dilation
we need would require
sitting in exactly that
forbidden zone.
By this logic,
our ocean world should
be impossible.
So how could a
stable ocean world exist?
The breakthrough came in
1963, when the New
Zealand mathematician Roy
Kerr solved Einstein's
equations for a spinning mass.
Real black holes are
not motionless, and many
of them spin,
some very fast.
A spinning Kerr black
hole drags space and
time around with it,
like honey being stirred
by a blender.
That dragging is called
frame-dragging, and it
creates a region called
the ergosphere.
Because space itself is
turning, the rules for
stable orbits are rewritten.
The closest safe orbit
moves much nearer to
the horizon, opening a
zone that was impossible
before.
But spinning alone is
not enough.
The black hole must
also be enormous.
Place a planet beside
a black hole of
ten Suns, and gravity
on the near side
would be far stronger
than on the far side.
These differences, called
tidal forces, would stretch
the planet like taffy
and tear it apart.
But if the black
hole holds a hundred
million Suns, its horizon
is so vast that
the pull changes only
gently across a planet.
The tidal forces become
survivable.
Calculations by Kip Thorne
for the film Interstellar
show how extreme the
recipe is.
You need a black
hole of about a
hundred million Suns,
spinning within one part
in a hundred trillion
of the fastest rate
physics allows, and a
planet orbiting right at
the edge of stability.
Astronomers think real
black holes are held
to somewhat slower spins
by their surroundings,
so this is the
extreme edge of what
the equations permit.
Edge or not,
they do not forbid it.
A planet balanced on
the very edge of destruction.
And that is only
the beginning of its
strange story.
