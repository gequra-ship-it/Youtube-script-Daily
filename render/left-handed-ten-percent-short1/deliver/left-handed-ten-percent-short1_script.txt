===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Among the Eipo of New Guinea, about twenty-seven percent of people were found to be left-handed. Among the Dioula of Burkina Faso, only about three percent. Why such a gap?

In 2005, two French researchers compared traditional societies and found a pattern. The more common homicide was, the more left-handers there were.

The idea is called the fighting hypothesis. Most fighters train against right-handers, so a left-hander's blows come from unfamiliar angles.

But the advantage only works while lefties are rare. If they became common, everyone would learn to fight them, and the surprise would vanish.

You can still see it in sports where you face an opponent, like boxing, fencing and tennis. There, left-handers are more common than their ten percent share.

It's a correlation, not proof. But it may help explain why one in ten of us is still left-handed. The full story is on Miya's Lab.
