Was the asteroid striking
a healthy planet alone,
or was Earth already
under strain?
Geologists have found
evidence of colossal
volcanic eruptions in what
is now India,
forming the massive Deccan
Traps.
Some scientists suspect
these vast lava flows
were releasing gases that
affected the climate before
the impact arrived.
Here it is important
to say what we
do not fully know.
Scientists agree that the
asteroid struck, and the
crater under the Gulf
of Mexico is the proof.
They still debate how
much the volcanic eruptions
in India contributed.
Some place more weight
on the volcanoes,
and others place more
weight on the impact itself.
That is a debate,
not a settled answer,
and the honest way
to tell this story
is to say so.
What everyone accepts is
that the combination of
crises was catastrophic
for life.
Think about how careful
scientists have to be.
A tempting story can
still be wrong.
The goal is not
to find an exciting answer.
The goal is to
find an answer that
survives every test you
can think of.
Finding the crater did
not close the case
on the volcanoes,
and finding the volcanoes
did not erase the crater.
Each clue had to
be tested against the others.
So why does this
debate matter?
Because it changes how
we think about extinction.
If the impact alone
did it, then the
dinosaurs were healthy
and simply unlucky.
If the volcanoes had
already weakened the world,
then the asteroid may
have been the final
push on a planet under strain.
Either way, the lesson
is the same.
A living world can
be fragile in ways
that are hard to
see until something goes
wrong.
A mountain from space
and a river of
lava might both have
mattered, and science is
still working out how
much each one did.
Next time you hear
that one single cause
explains a great event,
remember this story.
Sometimes the honest answer
is that we are
still finding out.
Watch the full story
to see how the
clues fit together.
