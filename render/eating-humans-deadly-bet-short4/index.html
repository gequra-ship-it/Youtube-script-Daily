Was eating other humans
ever a smart way
to survive starvation?
An archaeologist decided
to do the math.
Some researchers have
wondered whether early
humans turned to cannibalism
simply because they were
hungry.
It sounds plausible.
Survival is survival.
But in 2017,
the archaeologist James
Cole of the University
of Brighton decided to
test the idea,
in the journal Scientific
Reports.
He asked a simple question.
How many calories are
actually in a human body?
His calculation used an
adult man weighing about
sixty-six kilograms.
The whole body comes
to roughly one hundred
and forty-four thousand
calories.
The parts people would
normally eat add up
to about one hundred
and twenty-six thousand.
That sounds like a lot.
But look closer.
The skeletal muscle,
the actual meat,
is only about thirty-two
thousand calories.
The heart offers about
six hundred and fifty.
The kidneys, about three
hundred and eighty.
The brain and nerves,
about two thousand seven
hundred.
Measured against the work
of getting it,
that is a poor return.
Now compare it with
the prey of prehistoric
hunters.
Cole estimated that the
muscle of a mammoth
holds about three point
six million calories.
A woolly rhinoceros holds
about one point two
six million.
Even a red deer
holds about one hundred
and sixty-four thousand,
several times more than
a person.
Here is his own comparison.
Imagine a group of
twenty-five adults.
If their only food
were one human body,
the muscle would last
about half a day.
A mammoth could feed
the same group for
about two months.
So what did Cole conclude?
That the low food
value points toward social
or cultural explanations,
not purely nutritional ones.
This does not mean
it never happened.
Archaeologists have found
cut-marked human bones
at many ancient sites.
It means hunger alone
is a weak explanation.
There is one more cost.
Hunting a person is
far riskier than hunting
a deer, because people
fight back.
That is common-sense
inference, not a measured
result.
But add the disease
risk, and the picture
is clear.
The numbers say the
same thing as the biology.
It was a terrible deal.
