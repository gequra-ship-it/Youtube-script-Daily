What if the universe
is not just huge,
but growing, right now,
as you watch?
For a long time,
humanity took comfort in
the idea that even
if the universe was
immense, it was at
least static.
In a stationary universe,
if you were given
infinite time and immortal
patience, you could
eventually cross any
distance and reach the edge.
That comforting illusion
was completely demolished
in 1929 by an
astronomer named Edwin Hubble.
Pointing the giant 100-inch
Hooker telescope at the
distant heavens, Hubble
made a discovery that
sent shockwaves through
the foundations of science:
the universe is not
standing still.
It is expanding.
Hubble analyzed the light
coming from distant galaxies
and observed an undeniable
pattern: their light was
systematically shifted
toward the red end
of the electromagnetic
spectrum.
This redshift looks like
a Doppler effect,
and it was the
first clear sign of
a universe in motion.
The farther away a
galaxy was from us,
the faster it appeared
to be racing away.
This was not just
random movement of objects
drifting through empty space.
Every distant galaxy was
receding from every other
galaxy, like dots painted
on the surface of
an inflating rubber balloon.
Here is where human
intuition completely fails
and the true horror begins.
The galaxies are not
actually moving through
space like bullets flying
through the air.
Instead, the very fabric
of spacetime itself is
stretching between them.
Think of space not
as an empty stage
where actors dance,
but as a living,
dynamic fabric.
Between you and a
distant galaxy, new cubic
light-years of empty space
are constantly being
created, steadily pushing
everything farther apart
every single microsecond.
This is described by
Hubble's Law: recession
speed is proportional to
distance.
For every additional
megaparsec, roughly 3.26
million light-years, space
expands about 70 kilometers
per second faster.
A galaxy two megaparsecs
away recedes at about
140 kilometers per second.
At ten megaparsecs,
it is about 700.
The farther you look,
the faster the gap
between us multiplies.
Space itself is the
thing that is growing,
and that changes what
"reaching the edge" can
possibly mean.
