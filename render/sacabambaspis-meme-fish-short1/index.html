Look closely at this face.
Two round eyes sit
right at the front
of its head,
and below them a
small slit of a
mouth hangs open in
what looks like permanent
surprise.
It looks like a
clay project that went
slightly wrong.
But this is a
model of a real
animal, a fish called
Sacabambaspis, that lived
roughly four hundred and
sixty million years ago.
The model sits in
the Finnish Museum of
Natural History in Helsinki.
A photo of it
went viral on Twitter
in 2022, and in
2023 people in Japan
fell in love with it.
Fan art, comics,
stickers, plush toys,
and key chains followed.
The model was made
by the Estonian
palaeontologist Elga
Mark-Kurik for a fossil
fish exhibit that opened
in 1995.
She was a scientist,
not a professional sculptor.
According to BBC Science
Focus, she shaped it
from foam, cast it
in silicone, and gave
it a silvery glaze.
The eyes, reportedly,
came from a craft
store, and they were
googly doll's eyes.
Now to the famous face.
In the fossils,
the eyes sit at
the very front of
the head, close together,
each one supported by
a ring of bone.
Most fish today have
eyes on the sides
of the head,
which gives a wide
view of danger.
But the placement itself
comes from the fossils,
not from the sculptor.
There is one more catch.
Soft tissues like retinas
and lenses almost never
survive in fossils.
So nobody knows what
the eyes of a
living Sacabambaspis looked
like.
The model's round,
black-and-white stare,
the one that made
the internet laugh,
is the sculptor's choice.
The fossils tell us
where the eyes sat.
So how good is
the Helsinki model?
The overall plan is right.
It has a head
shield, a mouth slit,
closely spaced eyes at
the front, and no side fins.
The googly eyes and
the surprised look are
artistic choices.
It is a decent,
hand-made guess, and its
charm is partly an accident.
So the next time
that face appears in
your feed, smile,
but also look a little closer.
This is a real
animal, with a real
armor of bone,
and a place on
the family tree of
backboned life.
It was not a
joke of nature.
It was a successful
small fish in a
very different world.
