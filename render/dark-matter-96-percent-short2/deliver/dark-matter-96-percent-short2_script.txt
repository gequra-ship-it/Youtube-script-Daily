===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Why are galaxies not flying apart into total chaos right now? According to the established laws of physics, they should be. The story of how we found out begins with a simple question about speed.

In our solar system, distant planets drift lazily while inner worlds race around the Sun. Mercury speeds along at roughly forty-seven kilometers per second, while distant Neptune plods along at less than six. The farther out you go, the slower the orbit.

Astronomers expected the same rule in galaxies. According to the established laws of physics, stars near the outer rim of a galaxy should move much slower than stars near the crowded center.

Then, beginning around 1970, the astronomer Vera Rubin and her colleague Kent Ford measured how fast stars orbit in the Andromeda galaxy, and later in many other spiral galaxies. The outer stars were not slowing down. They were moving astonishingly fast, and their speeds stayed almost flat all the way to the edge of the visible disk.

These stars were moving so rapidly that the visible gravity of the galaxy could not possibly hold them. By all ordinary logic, these swirling galaxies should have torn themselves apart and flung their stars into the dark void long ago. Yet, they remain beautifully intact.

Scientists suspect that an immense, invisible halo of mysterious mass surrounds each galaxy, providing the extra gravitational glue needed to hold everything together. Because this substance emits no light, absorbs no light, and reflects no light, we simply call it dark matter.

Other evidence agrees. Massive clusters of galaxies stretch the light of galaxies far behind them into glowing arcs, and the bending is stronger than the visible matter can explain. The extra mass is clearly there. It simply does not shine.

Dark matter is not made of protons, neutrons, or electrons. If a cloud of it passed through your living room right now, you would not see it, hear it, or feel it. It interacts with our world almost entirely through gravity, yet its invisible grip controls the cosmos.

What could this mysterious phantom matter really be? Leading hypotheses suggest undiscovered subatomic particles, but no one knows for sure. Discover the full cosmic mystery right now on Miya's Lab.
