Neptune
Yet, as baffling as
the planet itself is,
Neptune’s largest satellite
tells an even more
dramatic tale.
Orbiting the ice giant
is Triton, a frozen
moon that stands as
one of the most
hostile and fascinating
bodies in the solar system.
Triton is about three
quarters the width of
our own Moon,
but it possesses one
extraordinary, glaring
anomaly.
It orbits Neptune in
the opposite direction
of the planet’s rotation,
a trajectory known as
a retrograde orbit.
No other large moon
in the entire solar
system circles its parent
world backward.
What does this backward
orbit tell us about
Triton’s history?
It suggests that Triton
was never born around
Neptune at all.
In the early days
of our solar system,
Triton was almost certainly
an independent dwarf planet,
wandering through the frigid
Kuiper Belt alongside worlds
like Pluto.
Long after the solar
system formed, Triton
ventured just a little
too close to the
gravitational reach of
giant Neptune.
Neptune’s immense gravity
snagged the passing world,
tearing it away from
its solo orbit and
locking it into an
eternal planetary orbit.
Triton’s backward dance
with Neptune, however,
is a tragedy with
a predestined conclusion.
Because Triton orbits in
retrograde, tidal
interactions are gradually
draining energy from the
moon’s orbital path.
Every single year,
Triton creeps a tiny
fraction closer to Neptune,
spiraling slowly inward
toward its colossal master.
Scientists calculate that
in a few billion
years, Triton will cross
a critical threshold known
as the Roche limit.
What will happen when
this frozen wanderer draws
too close?
When Triton breaches the
Roche limit, Neptune’s
ferocious tidal forces
will overcome the moon’s
self-gravity.
Triton will be ripped
apart at the seams,
fragmenting into countless
icy boulders, pebbles,
and glittering dust particles.
Over time, these shattered
remnants will spread out
along Triton’s former orbit,
forming a bright ring
system that may rival
the rings of Saturn today.
Neptune may one day
wear the crown of
the world it destroyed.
Triton will not last forever.
