For decades, one of
the first dinosaurs ever
named was drawn with
a horn on its nose.
The horn was actually
its thumb.
In the 1820s,
the English doctor Gideon
Mantell studied some fossil
teeth that looked like
a giant iguana's.
In 1825 he named
the animal Iguanodon.
Among the bones was
a cone-shaped spike.
Mantell guessed it was
a horn, and it
ended up on the
tip of the snout,
like a rhinoceros.
In 1854, London's Crystal
Palace sculptures showed
Iguanodon as a heavy
four-legged lizard.
You can still see
them in the park today.
Then, in 1878,
miners in Belgium found
dozens of skeletons.
The spike was on the hand.
It was a thumb,
and the animal could
stand on its hind legs.
And that was only
the beginning.
The full story of
how we kept redrawing
dinosaurs is on Miya's Lab.
