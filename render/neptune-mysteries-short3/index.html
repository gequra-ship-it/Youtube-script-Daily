Here lies what many
astronomers consider
Neptune’s greatest physical
riddle: the puzzle of
its internal heat.
When instruments measure
the thermal radiation
escaping from Neptune,
they reveal a shocking
discrepancy.
Neptune emits more than
two and a half
times as much heat
energy into space as
it absorbs from the
faint sunlight reaching it.
It is generating an
enormous reservoir of
internal thermal energy,
radiating warmth from its
deep interior into the
icy void.
Why should this be so unusual?
It becomes deeply unusual
the moment you compare
Neptune to its planetary
twin, Uranus.
Uranus and Neptune share
similar sizes, masses,
and atmospheric
compositions, making them
planetary siblings in almost
every measurable way.
Yet Uranus radiates very
little excess internal
heat at all,
sitting like a quiet,
frozen icebox in the dark.
What hidden fire is
burning inside Neptune
that seems to have
gone out inside Uranus?
Scientists have proposed
several fascinating
hypotheses to explain
Neptune’s restless internal
heat engine.
One hypothesis suggests
that Neptune is still
slowly radiating away the
primordial heat left behind
from its violent
gravitational birth four
and a half billion years ago.
During the formation of
the planet, countless rocky
and icy planetesimals
collided, releasing
gravitational potential
energy that became trapped
under thick, insulating
blankets of gas.
But why would Neptune
have retained this
primordial warmth while
Uranus somehow lost or
trapped its own?
Another idea is that
thermal convection inside
Neptune remains unimpeded,
allowing heat to escape
freely from the interior
to the surface.
Uranus might possess stable,
stratified layers that
act like giant thermal
blankets, trapping heat
deep within its core.
Alternatively, Neptune
might be experiencing active
chemical changes right
now in its deep interior.
Heavy materials may be
slowly sinking toward the
planetary center, converting
gravitational energy into
fresh thermal warmth.
This brings us to
one of the most
exotic ideas in modern
astrophysics: diamond rain.
Neptune keeps its secret.
