TENS OF MILLIONS OF YEARS
66 MILLION YEARS AGO
10 KM
Picture the Cretaceous
Period, when the age
of the dinosaurs reached
its peak.
The planet was a
warm greenhouse world with
no permanent ice caps
at the poles,
and oceans that flooded
the centers of continents
with warm, shallow seaways.
In this vibrant hothouse,
another revolutionary
miracle quietly bloomed
on the forest floor:
the very first flowering
plants.
For the first time
in Earth's history,
the world was decorated
with petals, drawing humming
insects into intricate
evolutionary partnerships.
Across this blooming
landscape strode legendary
titans, from the horned
herds of Triceratops to
the apex predator,
Tyrannosaurus rex, with
one of the strongest
bites of any land
animal known.
For tens of millions
of years, these magnificent
dynasties flourished, ruling
the land, while marine
reptiles and pterosaurs
ruled the seas and the sky.
Did they know the
sky was about to fall?
Sixty-six million years
ago, a mountain-sized
asteroid roughly ten
kilometers wide screamed
through the atmosphere
at cosmic speed,
slamming into the shallow
waters off Mexico's Yucatan
Peninsula.
The impact unleashed energy
equivalent to billions
of atomic weapons detonating
at once.
A blinding thermal pulse
may have set forests
ablaze across vast regions,
while global shockwaves
triggered gigantic tsunamis
and colossal earthquakes.
Trillions of tons of
vaporized rock and soot
soared into the
stratosphere, blocking
out the light of
the sun for years.
Without sunlight,
photosynthesis ground to
a halt, the global
food webs crumbled from
the bottom up,
and the non-avian dinosaurs
vanished forever into the
rock record.
What was it about
our small, furry ancestors
that allowed them to
outlast the monarchs of
the Mesozoic?
Scientists think small
mammals endured by
sheltering in burrows and
along riverbanks.
Because they were small,
warm-blooded, and able
to feed on decaying
organic detritus, seeds,
and insects, they weathered
the long impact winter
that starved the giant
reptiles.
When the skies finally
cleared and the sun
shone upon the blackened
Earth, the mammals crawled
out into a wide,
empty kingdom.
Tonight, you are the
long, quiet continuation
of that story.
