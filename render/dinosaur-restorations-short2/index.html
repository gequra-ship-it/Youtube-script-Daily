The original fossils of
Spinosaurus no longer exist.
They were destroyed in
a bombing raid in 1944.
In 1915, the German
paleontologist Ernst Stromer
described a huge predator
from Egypt, with spines
on its back at
least one point six
five meters long.
The fossils were kept
in a museum in Munich.
They were never moved
to safety, and on
a night in April
1944, the museum was
hit and the bones were lost.
For decades, artists drew
it as a typical
giant two-legged predator
with a sail.
In Jurassic Park Three,
it even beat a Tyrannosaurus.
Then new fossils from
Morocco showed short hind
legs and, in 2020,
a tall, paddle-like tail.
Some scientists think it
hunted in rivers.
Others disagree, and the
debate goes on.
The full story of
how dinosaurs keep being
redrawn is on Miya's Lab.
