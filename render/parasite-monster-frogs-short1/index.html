SUMMER 1995
Imagine a school field
trip on a warm summer day.
Students dip their nets
into a shallow marsh
and pull out something
that should not exist.
This happened in the
summer of 1995 in
rural Minnesota.
A group of students
on a biology field
trip was catching northern
leopard frogs.
But the frogs in
their nets had twisted
bodies, missing limbs,
and, in the most
terrifying cases, extra
legs growing out of
their abdomens.
Some of these frogs
had several extra legs
sprouting from the pelvic
region.
Others had no hind
legs at all and
had to crawl painfully
on tiny front arms.
A few were found
with legs growing backwards,
or jutting straight out
of the chest.
It looked as though
nature had suffered a
glitch and spawned real
monsters.
News of the multi-legged
frogs spread far beyond
Minnesota within weeks.
Alarm grew among wildlife
biologists, local
communities, and health
agencies.
If frogs in local
water were mutating this
badly, what might be
waiting for the people
who drank from the
same sources?
To a scientist,
the multi-legged frogs
were not just strange.
They were an alarm.
Frogs are famous for
being the first to
show that something is
wrong with the environment,
and many people assumed
that modern society had
poisoned the swamps with
artificial chemicals.
The first suspect was
human pollution.
Frogs have thin,
permeable skin that absorbs
water and air directly
from their surroundings,
which makes them famous
warning signs of
environmental trouble.
The suspect list included
agricultural pesticides,
radioactive waste, heavy
metals, and thinning ozone
letting in more ultraviolet
radiation.
Funding poured in.
Scientists exposed healthy
tadpoles to high
concentrations of various
pesticides and intense
ultraviolet radiation.
Some chemicals did cause
deformities, stunted growth,
or sudden death.
But none of them
reproduced the
hyper-specific, multi-legged
pattern seen in nature.
The chemical hypothesis
had hit a dead
end, and researchers were
baffled.
So what was really
happening in those ponds?
The answer would not
come from a sterile
laboratory.
It would come from the mud.
