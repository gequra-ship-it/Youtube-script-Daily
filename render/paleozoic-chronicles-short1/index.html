Meet Opabinia, one of
the strangest animals ever
found.
It lived about 508
million years ago,
in a sea that
is now part of
the Canadian Rockies,
preserved in rock called
the Burgess Shale.
It was only a
few centimeters long,
and it had five
eyes on stalks,
where most animals have
just two.
Instead of jaws,
it had a long,
flexible nozzle in front
of its head,
ending in a spiny claw.
Scientists think it grabbed
food with that claw
and passed it back
to its mouth.
Its neighbors were just
as strange.
Hallucigenia was first
drawn upside down.
And Anomalocaris had more
than 24,000 lenses in
a single eye.
This was the Cambrian
Explosion, when most major
animal groups first appeared.
The full story of
the Paleozoic is on
Miya's Lab.
