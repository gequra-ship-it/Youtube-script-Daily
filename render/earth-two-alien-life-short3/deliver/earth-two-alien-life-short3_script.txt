===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

If there may be billions of potentially habitable planets scattered across our galaxy, why is our night sky so terrifyingly silent? This haunting contradiction is known as the Fermi Paradox, and the scientific answer might give you chills. Our Milky Way is over thirteen billion years old, while Earth is only four and a half billion years old.

That means many potentially habitable planets may have formed billions of years before our Sun was even born. The Milky Way holds between one hundred billion and four hundred billion stars. If life emerges naturally, advanced civilizations had plenty of cosmic time to invent propulsion, explore the stars, and leave unmistakable technological signatures across the Milky Way.

Estimates suggest that a civilization with advanced propulsion might cross the entire galaxy in a few tens of millions of years, a mere blink of an eye in cosmic terms. Yet, when we point our most sensitive radio dishes toward deep space, we hear nothing but absolute, frozen silence.

To explain this eerie cosmic hush, scientists developed the Great Filter hypothesis. It suggests that somewhere between dead chemistry and an interstellar civilization, there exists a nearly insurmountable wall that wipes almost everyone out. The terrifying question is: where does this filter sit on the timeline?

On Earth, single-celled life ruled the oceans for over two billion years before two microbes merged to create the first complex cell. If the filter is behind us, it means the jump from inert chemicals to complex multicellular life is an extraordinarily rare miracle, perhaps one in a trillion, and we may be nearly alone in a sterile galaxy.

But if the Great Filter lies ahead of us, it means intelligent species arise all the time, only to destroy themselves through runaway technology, climate collapse, or planetary wars before they ever reach the stars. Discovering the remains of an extinct civilization on an alien world would be the most terrifying warning humanity could ever uncover.
