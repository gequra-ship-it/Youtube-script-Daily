What if the water
under the Sahara fell
as rain hundreds of
thousands of years ago?
Scientists found water
in the Nubian aquifer,
beneath Egypt's Western
Desert, that is two
hundred thousand to one
million years old.
How did it get
there, and what happens
when we pump it out?
Start underground.
Beneath the dunes lie
layers of rock laid
down over millions of years.
Sandstone is full of
tiny spaces between its
grains, and water can
soak in and slowly
move through.
A rock layer that
holds and passes water
like this is called
an aquifer.
But where did so
much water come from,
in a place this dry?
Roughly eleven thousand
to six thousand years
ago, the Sahara was
a green savanna with
lakes and rivers.
A stronger African monsoon
reached far north,
bringing regular summer rain.
Earth's orbit slowly
wobbles, and that changes
how much summer sunshine
falls on North Africa.
As the sunshine weakened,
the monsoon retreated south
and the Sahara dried out.
But water that soaked
into the ground during
this and earlier wet
periods stayed there.
Deep aquifers still hold it.
We call it fossil water.
How do we know its age?
Scientists measure rare
radioactive atoms dissolved
in the water.
In 2004, a team
used krypton-81 on water
from the Nubian aquifer
and found those ages.
And that water moves
north only about one
to two meters a year.
In the twentieth century,
powerful pumps changed
everything.
Deep wells could lift
fossil water far faster
than any natural spring.
In Saudi Arabia,
from the 1980s,
center-pivot sprinklers
turned desert into green
circles of wheat,
watered by ancient
groundwater.
But fossil water is
like money in a
bank account that no
longer receives deposits.
Every liter pumped from
a non-renewable aquifer
is a liter that
will not come back
in our lifetimes.
The most ambitious project
is Libya's Great Man-Made
River, begun in 1983.
It pumps fossil water
from deep in the
Sahara and carries it
north to coastal cities
through buried concrete
pipes four meters wide.
But that water comes
from aquifers that are
barely being refilled.
So the water under
the sand is real,
very old, and not
being refilled.
