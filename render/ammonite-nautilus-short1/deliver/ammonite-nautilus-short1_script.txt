===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Two spiral shells, one ancient and one alive today, look almost identical. But here is the surprise: ammonites and the nautilus were not close relatives. So why do they look so alike? Let's find out.

The nautilus belongs to the nautiloids, a branch of cephalopods that split off very early. Scientists place ammonites closer to modern squid, cuttlefish, and octopuses than to the nautilus. That means an ammonite was a closer relative of a squid than of a nautilus.

Their matching spirals are a classic case of convergent evolution, where separate lineages end up with a similar design. In nature, looking alike does not always mean being related, and this pair is one of the best examples.

Cut either shell in half, and the differences begin to show. Both are divided into chambers, linked by a thin tube called the siphuncle. It helps the animal adjust its buoyancy, a bit like the ballast tank of a submarine. In the nautilus, that tube runs through the middle of each chamber wall. In ammonites, it usually ran along the outer edge of the shell.

The chamber walls differ too. A nautilus builds simple, smooth, dome-shaped walls. Ammonites built walls with elaborate, fractal-like folds along their edges. Where those folds meet the shell, they leave wavy lines called sutures, which look like frozen leaves or snowflakes in stone.

Why did ammonites evolve such complicated patterns? Scientists are still debating it. One idea is that the folds helped strengthen thin shells against water pressure. Another is that they helped with the movement of liquid inside the chambers. No one knows for sure.

And ammonites were tough. About two hundred fifty-two million years ago, the worst mass extinction of all reduced them to only a few lineages, and they rebounded. They were hit hard again at the end of the Triassic, and recovered again.

So when ammonites vanished sixty-six million years ago, it was not because they were weak. The full story of the asteroid, the sunlit ocean, and the nautilus is on Miya's Lab.
