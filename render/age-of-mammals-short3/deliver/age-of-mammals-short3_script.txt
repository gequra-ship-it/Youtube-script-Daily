===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

Here is a strange fact. The largest animal that has ever swum in the sea began as a walking animal. A creature with four legs, that lived on land. So how does a land animal become a whale? The answer starts about fifty million years ago.

Sixteen million years earlier, an asteroid had ended the age of the dinosaurs. Mammals were spreading into the empty world, and their body sizes were expanding remarkably. Now the climate was climbing toward a thermal peak. This steamy greenhouse world is known as the Eocene epoch.

Along swampy coastlines, warm shallow bays opened between the trees. And in those bays, another strange transition began. Certain four-legged land mammals started to wade into the water.

Why did they go? Perhaps they were hunting slow fish. Perhaps they were escaping predators on land. Nobody can be sure which. But the water offered food and safety, and the habit stuck.

One early relative of whales is called Pakicetus. Its fossils, found in what is now Pakistan, are about fifty million years old. It walked on four legs. Yet its ear bones already resemble those of a whale.

Over generations, their bodies grew sleeker and their legs grew shorter. For an animal that spent more and more of its life in the water, a streamlined shape was a better fit than a land runner's build.

Their nostrils gradually migrated toward the top of their skulls. Over time, that shift became the blowhole. Now a whale can breathe at the surface while most of its body stays under the water.

Later whales, such as Basilosaurus, grew to about eighteen meters long. Yet they still carried tiny hind legs. They were a reminder of where their ancestors came from.

And here is the twist. The closest living relatives of whales are not other sea creatures. They are hippopotamuses. Two very different animals share one distant ancestor.

Millions of years later, those clumsy paddlers became majestic whales. Think about that breathtaking transformation for a quiet moment. A body made for walking became a body made for the open ocean.

Life constantly reshapes old tools to conquer entirely new realms. Front legs became flippers. A nose became a blowhole. Nature kept the parts it had, and changed what they were for.
