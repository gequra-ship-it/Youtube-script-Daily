Long before you ever
held a crayon,
you may have already
picked your favorite hand.
In ultrasound studies in
Belfast, about eighty-five
percent of fetuses at
ten weeks moved their
right arm more than
their left.
Later in pregnancy,
about ninety-five percent
were seen sucking their
right thumb.
Years later, the researchers
checked on some of
these children.
All sixty right-thumb
suckers had grown up
right-handed.
Of the fifteen left-thumb
suckers, ten were
left-handers.
And it may not
even start in the brain.
In 2017, scientists in
Germany found that genes
in the fetal spinal
cord were more active
on one side,
as early as the eighth week.
So the hand you
write with may have
been chosen before your
first breath.

one in ten of
