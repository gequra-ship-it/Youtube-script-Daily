About 252 million years
ago, most life on
Earth disappeared.
And one of the
big winners looked a
bit like a pig with tusks.
In Siberia, enormous
eruptions poured out
millions of cubic kilometers
of lava, and the
magma burned through buried
coal.
Tropical seas warmed to
near 40 degrees Celsius,
and much of the
ocean lost its oxygen.
Roughly 80 percent or
more of marine species
vanished, along with about
70 percent of land
vertebrate species.
Even the trilobites were
gone forever.
But Lystrosaurus, a stocky
plant-eater with a beak
and two tusks, survived.
It probably lived in
burrows all its life,
and may have grown
fast and eaten almost
any plant it found.
In the earliest Triassic
rocks of South Africa,
it makes up more
than 90 percent of
the vertebrate fossils.
The full story is
on Miya's Lab.
