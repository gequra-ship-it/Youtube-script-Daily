MILLIONS OF KM
Imagine falling toward
a supermassive black hole
while a friend watches
from far away.
Here is what each
of you sees.
From the perspective of
your friend safely watching
through a telescope millions
of kilometers away,
your journey seems to
slow down.
As you approach the
boundary known as the
event horizon, they notice
your movements becoming
strangely sluggish.
Every wave of your
hand takes longer and
longer to complete.
Your ship does not
crash into a wall
or plunge inward at
blinding speed.
Instead, to your friend’s
eyes, you appear to
decelerate as if you
were moving through
invisible, thickening
molasses.
Then comes the light.
Every beam of light
your suit emits must
fight against the crushing
gravitational pull to escape
toward the mother ship.
As that light struggles
upward, its wavelengths
get stretched out,
losing energy in a
process called gravitational
redshift.
To your friend,
your white spacesuit begins
to shift toward a
dull orange, then a
deep crimson red.
The flashes from your
beacon grow dimmer and
dimmer, arriving later
and later at the telescope.
Eventually, the wavelength
of your light stretches
so far that it
slips entirely out of
the visible spectrum.
Your image shifts into
infrared, then fades into
faint radio waves,
until the last detectable
photon finally registers
on their instruments.
To the outside observer,
you never actually cross
the event horizon.
You simply freeze at
the threshold of the
abyss, growing redder,
fainter, and more ghostly,
until your image fades
into permanent darkness.
In their timeline,
your crossing would take
an infinite amount of
time to observe.
How can that be
true if you are
the one falling?
What do you actually
experience as you look
down at the horizon
with your own eyes?
For you, floating freely
in your suit,
time does not slow
down at all.
Your wristwatch ticks with
the exact same steady
rhythm it had when
you were sitting at
home on Earth.
Your heart beats normally,
and your lungs draw
breath at their usual pace.
As far as your
personal timeline is
concerned, nothing freezes,
and you do not
wait an eternity.
You fall smoothly,
accelerating steadily toward
the dark veil ahead.
Two people, two clocks,
one fall.
