TYPE 0.7
What would life inside
a Type One civilization
actually look like?
It means capturing every
single photon of sunlight
falling on the world,
along with all wind,
tidal, and geothermal power.
Can you imagine waking
up in a world
where extreme weather is
simply scheduled?
A Type One society
wouldn't just survive on
their planet; they would,
in this speculative picture,
manage it like a
living organism.
In this imagined picture,
earthquakes might be defused
before they strike,
hurricanes might be
dissipated, and global
climates might be kept
in perpetual balance.
So, where exactly do
we stand on this
monumental ladder?
You might assume our
glowing cities and orbital
satellites place us
comfortably near Type One,
but the real number
is humbling.
Astronomer Carl Sagan
estimated that humanity
sits somewhere around Type
zero point seven.
Could an asteroid or
a sudden solar storm
still knock out our
digital networks tomorrow?
A severe one could.
We still burn fuels
made from ancient buried
life, and an unexpected
shift in our climate
can still bring our
supply chains to their knees.
Notice the contrast between
how we feel and
where we stand.
We feel advanced because
our technology looks
miraculous to us.
We split atoms,
dam great rivers,
and beam data through space.
Yet on the cosmic
scale, we have only begun.
This is not meant
to be discouraging.
A ladder with many
steps ahead is also
a ladder with a
great deal of room to grow.
A single bad turn
of events could still
break our networks,
and our daily lives
still rest on fuels
made from ancient buried life.
The number zero point
seven is a polite
way of saying we
have a long way to go.
Be clear, too,
that the Type One
picture of controlled
weather is an exercise
of imagination, built on
the idea of using
all the energy that
reaches a planet from
its star, along with
its wind, tides,
and heat from within.
Nobody has lived in
such a world.
