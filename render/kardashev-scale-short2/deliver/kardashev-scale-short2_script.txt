===== 1부: TTS 입력용 전문 (장면당 한 문단) =====

What would it look like if a civilization outgrew its entire planet? To picture that, look up at our Sun.

In just one second, our Sun releases far more energy than humanity has used in its entire recorded history. Every lump of coal, every barrel of oil, every wind turbine and nuclear plant, added together, is dwarfed by a single second of sunlight. And almost all of that light simply streams out into cold, empty space.

Kardashev's scale ranks civilizations by power. A Type One civilization commands the energy of a whole planet. A Type Two commands the energy of a whole star, billions of times more than Type One. That is a huge jump, and it asks a hard question. How would anyone catch that much starlight?

In nineteen sixty, physicist Freeman Dyson suggested an answer. An advanced civilization might one day want that wasted light. His idea was not a solid shell, but a vast swarm of solar collectors orbiting a star. The idea became known as the Dyson sphere.

Imagine a swarm of mirrored collectors so large that together they form a glowing shell around a sun. A civilization that built one could capture most of the light its star sends out. With that much energy, scarcity on a single planet would matter far less.

What could you do with that much power? In theory, a Type Two civilization might move planets, run computers far beyond anything we can build, or keep huge numbers of orbital habitats supplied with energy. Some dangers that threaten us, like ice ages, would matter far less. Others, such as the aging of the star itself, would not go away.

To us, building something that dwarfs a planet sounds impossible. But no known law of physics forbids it. It is a question of materials, robotics, and enormous amounts of time. Astronomers have searched for the infrared glow that such structures would give off. So far, none has been confirmed.
