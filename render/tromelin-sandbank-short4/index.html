How do you survive
on an island with
no trees, no soil,
and no river?
You begin by studying
what the island does offer.
To understand how anyone
survived, we have to
look at what this
island did offer.
There were no edible
plants, but the island
was a nesting ground
for seabirds.
Many thousands of them,
mostly sooty terns,
laid eggs on the sand.
Sea turtles hauled
themselves onto the beaches
at night to lay theirs.
Birds, eggs, turtle meat,
and some fish became
the foundation of the
castaways' diet.
Food alone is not enough.
On a sand island,
fire is life.
Without fire, meat cannot
be cooked, warmth cannot
be found on cold
nights, and the days
stay dark.
Archaeologists later found
flint, fire strikers,
and nails from burned
ship timber.
Historians report that
the survivors kept a
fire burning for fifteen
years, fed by driftwood
and pieces of the wreck.
We do not know
every detail of how
they did it.
But their rescuers found
a fire still lit.
Think about the dedication
that took.
There were no forests
to cut for firewood.
Someone had to walk
the beaches every day,
collecting whatever the
ocean left behind.
Fifteen years is more
than five thousand days.
Archaeologists who dug
on Tromelin between 2006
and 2013 found something
extraordinary.
These people did not
just huddle in the sand.
They built a settlement.
At the highest point
of the island,
they raised low,
sturdy buildings from beach
sandstone slabs set upright
and topped with coral blocks.
Low walls helped protect
them from the wind
that sweeps the island.
Even the food leaves a trace.
In 2013, the team
recovered about eighteen
thousand bird bones,
most of them from
sooty terns, along with
bones of turtles and fish.
It is a record
of how people ate
on an island with
nothing else to eat.
The excavations also
uncovered copper bowls
and utensils from the wreck.
These were not left to rust.
The survivors hammered
the copper and repaired
their cookware again and
again, some pieces as
many as eight times
over the years.
In a place with
no new materials,
every scrap of metal mattered.
They did not just survive.
They built a society.
