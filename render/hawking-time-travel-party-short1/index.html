1992
Champagne on ice,
balloons on the ceiling,
and a banner reading
"Welcome Time Travellers."
Nobody came, and that
was the point.
On June twenty-eighth,
2009, at noon Universal
Time, Stephen Hawking held
a reception at Gonville
and Caius College in
Cambridge.
It looked like a
normal party, but the
guests of honor were
people from the future.
It was one of
the strangest experiments
in physics.
Here is the catch.
Hawking did not send
a single invitation before
the party.
He waited until it was over.
Only then did he
publish the invitation,
with the date,
the time, and the
map coordinates of the room.
His logic was simple.
Suppose time travel is
invented someday.
A future traveler could
find that invitation in
a record of the
past, set a machine
to the right place
and moment, and walk
through the door.
The party would be
full of visitors from
centuries ahead.
But the room stayed empty.
Hawking told the story
in his 2010 television
series, Into the Universe
with Stephen Hawking.
He said this was
experimental evidence that
travel into the past
is impossible.
Now, it was a
joke, and a good
skeptic could poke holes
in it.
Maybe future people never
saw the invitation.
Maybe they found our
century boring.
Maybe they cannot reach
a single room in
a single moment of history.
The party proves nothing
by itself.
It was a charming
way to make a point.
The serious physics behind
it is stranger.
Einstein's equations of
general relativity seem
to allow loops in
spacetime, paths that lead
back to your own past.
For decades, physicists
asked whether nature has
some rule that forbids them.
Hawking thought it does.
In 1992, he proposed
what he called the
Chronology Protection
Conjecture.
It says the laws
of physics stop time
machines from ever being
built.
In his words,
there seems to be
a chronology protection
agency that makes the
universe safe for historians.
The word to notice
is conjecture.
It is Hawking's proposal,
not a proven theorem.
Many physicists find it
convincing, but it still
depends on a theory
of quantum gravity that
nobody has yet finished.
So the empty room
is not a proof.
It is a clue,
left by a scientist
with a sense of humor.

why nature might block
the past is on
